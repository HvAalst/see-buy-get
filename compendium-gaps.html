Built today: the global barcode and the commercial invoice

The two gaps you noticed, built for a shipper's loads in the Business app. Commit 349349d.

Built

The global barcode, beside ours

A business gives its GS1 company prefix under Business details, with the extension digit it sets aside for labels printed here (an owner sets it; staff never change it). Each package of a load it then posts gets its own SSCC under that prefix, never given twice, and kept with the load. The labels, one 100 × 150 mm label a package, carry our code on top (the QR to the load's tracking page) and the GS1-128 barcode with the SSCC below: what a receiving warehouse scans into its own system. Without a prefix the label is ours alone; we never make a prefix up, because it would be some real company's. The consignment note lists the SSCCs (box 6) and the number of packages (box 7).

Checked: every barcode as drawn on the labels was decoded by an independent reader (ZXing) and matched the number printed under it; the check digits match GS1's published examples.

Built

The commercial invoice, signed, in three copies

On a load that crosses a border, the business writes its commercial invoice: its own number and date, the buyer, the money, why the goods cross (sale, sample, gift, repair, return), and line by line the description, quantity, net weight, value per item, country of origin and HS code. Ticking the declaration signs it, by name and time, with a fingerprint printed on every copy and a line for a hand signature. It prints as three copies, each saying which it is, with “all 3 copies go in a clear pouch on the outside of package 1”; package 1's label says so too, and box 5 of the consignment note lists it. Once signed it never changes: a new one takes its place and the old one is kept, marked.

Your rule holds: every HS code and value is the shipper's own. Nothing suggests a code, and the invoice says “SeeBuyGet does not classify goods”. Staff and carriers read it; only the business that posted the load writes it.

Built

A load that crosses a border

Found while building: the Business app posted every load with both ends in the business's own country, so a shipper could not send anything abroad from it. The form now asks which country it goes to, and says when it crosses a border.

Open

What these two do not cover yet

A store's parcel crossing a border goes with the same commercial invoice now (round 13, edbb110); a store's labels have no GS1 code yet. An expeditor's client cannot give its own prefix, so a client's goods carry our code alone. Scanning an SSCC at our counters does not find the load yet. The label prints as a page; there is no thermal (ZPL) stream.

Built

No papers, no loading

Your answer: refuse, unless the shipper's own paper travels. A business's load crossing a border is not taken over until its commercial invoice is signed here, or the shipper says, by name, that its own paper invoice travels with the goods (box 5 then says whose paper it is). The driver's load card and pickup sheet say so before the drive to the gate; the refusal at the gate is the first thing asked. Counter bookings and stores' diverted orders are not held to it: nobody in the app could write their invoice. Commit 2bf4ada.

Wrong, not just missing

Things the check found that are wrong as they stand: a rule of yours broken, or a figure that misleads. Three were small and plainly against your rules, so they are fixed; the rest are yours to decide.

Fixed Commit 70194c5.

Shopkeepers' and drivers' phone numbers were public

The public route that resolves an identity (GET /v1/did/:id) returned its “controller”, which for a shop, a driver and a fleet is pudo:<their mobile number>; shops' identities are listed on the public network map. And anyone could mint an identity (POST /v1/did) outside a sandbox. A list of shopkeepers' numbers invites SIM-swap and mobile-money fraud.

Fixed Commit 49b4563.

Ratings were lost at every restart

Shop and driver ratings were kept in memory only, while their average was stored. Every deploy wiped them, reset each driver's count, and lifted the bar that keeps a driver rated below 2.5 off freight.

Fixed Commit 0aea927.

The public store page counted reports as upheld claims

It counted every damage report that closed itself (a report, judged by nobody) as “a claim we upheld”, against your rule that damage is reported, not judged.

Fixed Commit bc494d2.

The old M2 engine suggests HS codes

It fills in an HS code from the description when none is typed, serves a “suggest a code” route, and guesses UN numbers for dangerous goods from free text: what your “never classify” decision rules out. It is closed outside a sandbox, but it is live code.

Your answer: removed. A typed HS code is still checked for its format; a declared UN number is still looked up and checked against the DGR ceiling.

Fixed Commit c6ac473.

“DDU” on every parcel declaration

A parcel crossing a border is declared under “DDU”, which is not an Incoterms 2020 term (it was dropped in 2010; DAP took its place) and is not in our own catalogue of the eleven. The declared customs value is also the order's subtotal, while cover uses the value the store declared.

Your answer: DAP, at the shop the parcel is collected from, and one value: the store's declared figure, or what the buyer paid when it gives none.

Fixed Commit ad3a235.

The shop sign-up form answered for the applicant

It sent “Android 13”, “reliable power”, 08:00–18:00 every day, a licence number made from the ID, and a clean criminal record for everybody, so the checks behind the franchise requirements always passed.

Your answer: ask the applicant. The form now asks the phone's Android version, whether the shop has reliable power, its trading licence number, its opening hours, and a declaration that the owner's record is clean. The button says which one is still unmet. Staff see the answers beside each application in Joining the network.

Fixed Commit e103ece.

One person ran a company's payouts

Every other act on money or identity by staff took two people; running a payout took one.

Your answer: a second person approves. One member of staff raises a payout run; a different one approves it and sends it to the payment rail. The same person is refused, and the money screen says who raised it and who approved it.

Harmless today

The digital twin marks hub parcels as exceptions

A parcel through a hub is handed to a bus twice; the tracking engine calls that an illegal step and the twin marks the parcel as an exception. Nothing reads the twin, so nobody sees it, but it matters the day the console does.

The gaps that matter most

Of everything missing or thin, these would matter most for a network starting in Kenya, in the order I would take them. All eleven are built, each with what you answered and what was built. What comes next is asked with my recommendations.

  1. The counter without signal. Release, sign-in and top-up need a connection; a take-in cannot finish offline because its photo must upload first; breakdown and hazard reports are not queued. First step built (79c96dc): a take-in finishes with no signal, its photo held on the phone and sent first when the signal returns; its label and release code show once when it arrives. Built (00d2e5b): a driver's breakdown and road-hazard reports (and a word on a hazard) wait on the phone with their photos and go when the signal returns, dated when they were made, the arrival kept beside it. Sign-in (driver, shop, hub) and a shop's top-up say they need a signal before anybody tries. Walking it found that a washout, a closed road or a checkpoint could never be reported at all (fixed in the same commit).
  2. Flags are not cases. The automatic flags (stagnant, ghost bus, offline shop) are recomputed on every read: no severity, no owner, no acknowledgement, no history, no escalation when nobody acts. Your answer: this next. Built (32ac9a1). Every flag opens a case with a severity, in the console's new Cases screen. The list is the most pressing first; the kinds above it count all of them and filter to one. Escalation shows only in the console: the console has no notifications.

Never sent to Safaricom yet; its charge is not booked; a run is still pressed by hand.
  4. Who is at the counter. A shop signs in from any browser and can scan from home; there is no device binding, no ID-registry or sanctions check, and no counter-staff logins, so the owner's PIN gets shared. Your answer: this next, and staff sign in with "own phone + PIN". Built. A shop's sign-in works only on its own phones: a new phone or browser is confirmed once by a code texted to the shop's number, and the owner sees and forgets them in Settings (e9ed1de). Out by your decisions

Compendium requirements the check found declined by a decision you took. Listed so they are not mistaken for gaps.

Classification and duty

An HS-code lookup, duty in the all-in price, dangerous-goods auto-classification (M2-024, M3-043, M2-033). The shipper's own HS code is now recorded on its invoice; nothing suggests one.

Holding other people's money

Escrow on a match, staged funding, freezing money over a dispute, factoring and earned-wage access (SSE-010, 012, 051, 061, 062). Business loads settle between the parties; parcels are pre-paid.

Judging disputes

Manual and tiered dispute resolution (PL-8b, SSE-053): every reported problem ends in a damage claim report the claimant hands on.

Texting receivers

Tracking SMS, OTP by SMS, an address check by SMS, re-sending either (chapters 13–16, Appendix C). The receiver follows the parcel in the app.

The old control tower

Disruption feeds, the response recommender, shipper policies, what-if simulation, autonomous action and rollback (M7-021, 030, 031, 040–042, 050–053): parked.

The counter's own rules

An admin overdraft per shop (one standing 20-parcel credit line instead), staff editing a shop's record, a counter with no settings screen (one Settings entry in every app), cash on delivery.

Biggest gaps

  • Flags are not cases. The ten time-window flags are recomputed on every read, with no severity, owner, acknowledgement or history, and no escalation when nobody acts. Once there are dozens of shops, an operator cannot tell which stuck parcel someone is already chasing.
  • No automatic watch on where vehicles are. Ghost Bus is judged by time on bus alone.   • Proof of delivery is thin and anchors do not last. There is no GPS and no signature, and the photo at parcel collection is optional. The "chain" is in server memory, so the tamper-evidence goes at the next restart. Cash-heavy, low-trust markets settle disputes on exactly this evidence.

Chapter 9 — SSE Settlement and Escrow

How escrow is treated: parcels are pre-paid into a shop or store deposit held in the sister company's books, so no escrow exists by design (graduation.service.ts:19 deliberately emits no match.confirmed). A store's heavy freight is billed from the store deposit when a driver claims the load, and the driver is paid on delivery. A business's load is stamped settlement: 'between_parties' (dispatch.service.ts:555) and builds up nothing on our books (:2914); its price is a Quote (quote.types.ts:10, "a record and never a posting"). Load claims follow the same damage rule. Shop-counter loads and co-op lots still build up a carrier payable while the customer pays the carrier in cash; this is an open question on progress.html, raised 2026-10-04.

Biggest gaps

  • Shop and driver ratings are lost on every restart. They are held only in memory (be/modules/pudo/rating.service.ts:71-75), while the average copied to the shared store survives. A restart therefore wipes every rating and silently lifts the freight bar on poorly rated drivers, and two figures for the same person disagree.
  • No way to actually pay providers out. The only payout rail is a sandbox that cannot settle (payouts.module.ts:15), no mobile-money payout adapter exists in code, and nothing runs on a schedule. Couriers who live on daily cash get "same-day capable", not same-day.
  • The public store page miscounts claims. It counts every self-closed "report only" damage case as "a claim we upheld" (merchant-rating.service.ts:158, en.home.ts:16). Since no company may state cover, every reported problem lands there, which misstates merchants and contradicts the reported-not-judged rule.
  • No fiscal e-invoicing and no withholding tax. eTIMS: built for Kenya, not yet sent to KRA (77dec19). VAT is collected to tax payable, but no eTIMS or EFD invoice is issued, although the country catalogue lists eTIMS as a Kenyan obligation (catalogue.data.ts:293). A VAT-registered Kenyan sister cannot trade lawfully without it. Withholding tax: not built.
  • Only one funding rail, M-Pesa. One provider per deployment (funding.module.ts), with no MTN, Airtel or Telebirr. Uganda, Tanzania and Ethiopia, the next markets, cannot top up except through staff confirming a statement.
  • No appeal for a driver barred by ratings. A driver kept off freight by ratings cannot contest one, and the operator's below-floor review queue has a route but no screen (pudo.controller.ts:4120). For informal carriers that is income lost with no recourse.

Biggest gaps

  • A store can lose a status callback without knowing. Answered (5d8df21b): the Store app says what became of each, and the store sends one again. After eight tries over about two days, a ready_for_pickup (the message that carries the collection code) is marked failed. The Store app does not show whether a callback arrived, and no one can send it again. Small-shop endpoints go down often, and a buyer who is never told the parcel is waiting leaves it to run into holding fees and a return.
  • Rate limits are per process, by IP when no key is sent, and absent on signed-in routes. Mobile users in Kenya and Ethiopia sit behind shared carrier addresses, so the anonymous quote button can throttle a whole operator's customers at once. A second server instance would also double every limit.
  • A leaked secret key is noticed only by the store. There are no usage counts per key, no request log, no alert and no automatic suspension. A secret key mints shipments charged to the store's prepaid float, so a leak can drain a small merchant's money before anyone looks.
  • The sandbox cannot exercise the callback flow. Test-key shipments are never created, and there is no way to push a state or reset. Merchants go live without ever seeing a real ready_for_pickup reach their handler, so integration bugs show up on real buyers' parcels.
  • Integration tooling is thin. There are only curl examples and one Node snippet: no packaged client or shop-platform plug-in, no docs page in production, no change log and no deprecation notice. Small merchants mostly integrate through plug-ins, and an unannounced change to a /v1 route would break their checkouts silently.

Biggest open risks for a network starting in Kenya

  • The counter does not work without signal. Release, sign-in, pay and chat are online-only, as offline-story.md says. On top of that, the take-in cannot finish offline because its required photo must upload first. Breakdown and hazard reports are not queued either. With no SMS or USSD fallback (risks 10, 52, 54), a shop in a weak-signal area loses both of its main jobs while the signal is down.
  • Data loss on restart, and backups that sit on the same disk. Shop and driver ratings are in memory only (pudo/rating.service.ts:71-75), so each deploy wipes them and lifts the low-rating freight pause. Driver offers are also memory-only. Database dumps are taken per release on the same server disk (04_platform/scripts/deploy.sh:76). Off-server backups are a manual step (04_platform/DEPLOY.md:323-337). The phone's offline queue lives in localStorage and dies if the browser is cleared.
  • Cash and float sit with the shopkeeper. Walk-up senders and late collectors pay the shop in cash, so the shop carries fake notes and robbery risk. Shops can run 20 parcels on credit. Paying out to shops and drivers only has a sandbox rail with no mobile-money adapter, and someone has to press Run (be/foundation/payouts/payouts.module.ts:15). Same-day pay is therefore not something the code can deliver yet.
  • Fraud and abuse at the counter go unchecked. There is no shelf audit or random photo request (24). There are no separate counter-staff logins, so the owner's PIN gets shared (29). Abuse at the counter cannot be reported and no customer is banned (45). Because ratings are lost on restart, a bad actor's record does not last.
  • Literacy and language. Read-aloud and icons exist, but Swahili has about 20 strings out of roughly 10,000, so the voice mostly speaks English to shopkeepers who may not read it (21, 27).
  • Driver safety. There is no SOS or panic action. Nothing watches PUDO drivers for leaving their route or speeding (34, 36). A breakdown report needs signal at the roadside.
