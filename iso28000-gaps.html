The short answer

The chain of custody on a load is strong; the risk model, the hubs' receipt of bags, and the depth of partner vetting are not. Certification is scoped and organisational as much as technical, and the comment's own advice — enforce the steps in the app, certify the core and the hubs first — fits how the product is already built.

Strong

Integrity of a load

ISO 17712 seal classes, three photographs per seal, the number read blind off the door at pickup (a wrong one and the load does not leave), customs re-seals at a post, a broken seal reported with cause, photo, official paper and place; the securement photo and the load-safety checklist gate pickup; no papers, no loading across a border; since round 31 every evidence photo is the taker's own; since round 35 a seal not as written, a broken seal or a cut wrap holds the load until operations releases it. Each step is dated by the phone, works without signal and is fingerprinted on the frozen consignment note.

Thin

Parcels through hubs, and partners

Since round 36 a bag leaves closed — a coded seal or a photograph — and is received on it: the number read off the seal, a photograph, before it is opened; since round 37 the driver taking it reads the seal and photographs it too, and a bag a counter loaded onto them is received nowhere until they have. Since c410dd4 a hub that signs itself up is vetted from its papers before it works, as a shop is. Light-vehicle couriers start without KYC, and no partner signs a security declaration. Nothing caps what a new or informal partner may hold.

Missing

A risk model

No hub, shop, corridor or route carries a security risk level or category (pilferage, hijacking, unrest, roadblock extortion), and no countermeasure is tied to one. Incidents — a broken seal, a vehicle standing still too long, a closure — reach people, but change no risk anywhere.

Wrong, not just missing

Found while checking, and confirmed in the code. The first two were holes in controls that looked shipped; your answer: "Fix both now". Both are fixed.

Fixed

The driver could read the seal numbers before pickup

The driver who claimed the load is a party to it, and a party read GET dispatch/freight/:id/seals and the consignment note, both carrying the numbers; the tracking page, whose key is on the load's labels, said them too. Now, until the goods are taken over, the carrier reads which seals and what they are, never their numbers; the note says "its number is read off the door at taking over"; the tracking page says no seals. The shipper that wrote them, and staff, read them always.

Fixed

A closed road or border did not stop a load

ROAD_CLOSED and BORDER_CLOSED were declared as blocking with no branch in the route check, so a load on a road reported closed was offered as if nothing were there. Now both block, a report somebody disputes still blocks and says so, and any blocking kind without a sentence of its own blocks too, so none is dropped in silence again.

Need to know

The commercial invoice is open to the carrier

The invoice — line values, the total, exporter and importer — is read by every party to the load, the claiming driver and their fleet included; the consignment note gives the driver the goods' description and the carriage amount. The load card itself shows neither.

Fixed

Any shop could read any parcel's history

GET parcels/:id answered any shop with the scans and photo ids. Now (dff59ce, without asking: it is your rule that a number opens nothing) a shop reads the whole history only of a parcel taken in there or addressed there; of one on a bus, which any counter may be handed, only what it needs to take it in; of anybody else's, nothing.

Limit

The offline seal check can be worked backwards

So the phone can check a reading without signal, it holds a fingerprint of load and number. A seal number is short, so a phone can try them all. With the numbers no longer readable elsewhere before pickup, this is the one way left, and it takes deliberate work; it is not a secret on its own.

What I would take first

  1. Close the two holes. Seal numbers withheld from the carrier until the goods are taken over; a closed road or border blocks a load. Done: 80bb1a4.
  2. The tamper protocol. A seal not as written, a broken seal or a cut wrap holds the unit, logs the exception against it and its SSCC, and tells operations before custody is taken. Done: a969620, dbd7c1e.
  3. Receiving at hubs and shops. Our own bags sealed with a real number; "Receive" asks for the number, a photo and the scan. Done: 48d1697 — both ways of closing wired in, the coded seal suggested.
  4. A driver takes a bag on its seal. The number read off it, a photograph as taken, as it was closed — part of the load, or read afterwards when a counter loads it by the driver's pass; received nowhere until then. Done: ad4f923.
  5. Need-to-know. The carrier sees what to carry and where, not what it is worth. A decision first: the invoice travels with the goods for customs.
  6. A risk register. Each hub, shop and corridor rated, with its countermeasures, fed by incidents. The foundation pillar; larger, and some of it is yours to judge.
  7. Partners. A security declaration at sign-up, hubs vetted, a custody cap for the new and informal. Hubs vetted: done, c410dd4, without asking. The declaration and the cap are yours to decide (below).
  8. The trail. Anchor custody, loads, seals and the documents' fingerprints; staff roles and a log of what staff open. The log of what staff open: done, 94396bd; anchoring loads, note copies, exceptions and bags: done, a47cb90 — both without asking. Narrower roles and an anchor outside our database are yours to decide.
  9. At the checkpoint. Held by police or customs as a state; a driver's line to a central desk; the papers kept on the phone. Held as a state: done, 2ef0d7e, without asking. The papers on the phone: done, 432b632. The line to a desk needs your number.

Not software

Organisational

Scope of certification

Certify the platform and the hubs first, with the shops as monitored providers under the app's hand-over controls — the comment's advice, and what the product's controls already lean towards.

Organisational

AEO and trusted-trader status

Registering with customs once certified. The product can hold the status and its papers once you have them; it cannot apply.

Physical

Fences, zones and waiting areas

Segregated docks and waiting areas are built, not coded. The product can name who may enter and keep who did.
