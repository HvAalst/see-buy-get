BondBridge's construction — overlapping letters on a rounded tile — carrying the three words of the name. They grow left to right, so the monogram reads as a journey rather than an abbreviation: see furthest back, get arrived and in front.

Anatomy

Three letters, one baseline

The letters share a baseline and grow — 58, 78, 100 — so the tops rise to the right. Depth is carried by colour as well as size: navy recedes, sand sits between, white comes forward.

see · 58 buy · 78 get · 100
100-unit tile · overlap 42% · radius 20%

see

Smallest, furthest back, deep navy. Mostly hidden behind what follows it — which is the point: it is the beginning of something, not the thing itself.

buy

The middle beat, in Sand — a tint of the tile's own orange. It has to separate from both neighbours without competing with either.

get

Largest, white, fully in front. Bricolage's capital G carries a straight spur that cuts a hard diagonal corner — it reads as a paper plane, which is the send icon the whole network is built around.

Scale

Clear space and minimum size

Clear space on all four sides is x — a quarter of the tile. Nothing enters it, including the edge of the page.

Clear space · x = ¼ tile

At 16 px the tile resolves to a white G with colour behind it, which is the correct reading — the same way BondBridge's two B's resolve to one. Below 110 px wide, drop the wordmark and use the tile alone rather than setting type nobody can read.

Misuse

Four ways to break it

✕

Don't set the wordmark in orange. Navy on light grounds, white on dark, nothing else.

✕

Don't stretch, condense or rotate. Scale the whole lockup proportionally.

✕

Don't add shadows, glows, gradients or outlines. The tile is flat.

✕

Don't crowd it. Keep x of clear space on every side.
