**Bottom line for the design**
- **No East African or neighbouring state is in OTIF (CIM) or SMGS.** So CIM cannot apply to East African rail by law. Art. 1 §2 opt-in needs one end in a Member State.
- **East African rail freight is national law plus each railway's own conditions.** The Northern and Central Corridor agreements only commit states to accept each other's rail waybill.
- **CIM itself allows the electronic note directly (Art. 6 §9). It needs no protocol, unlike e-CMR.** OTIF is now drafting amendments to cover electronic documents fully and expressly; none is in force.

## 2. CIM Art. 1, scope (verbatim, OTIF text)

**Your paragraph numbers are off by one.** §2 is the opt-in, §3 is road or inland-waterway legs, §4 is sea.

- **§1** "These Uniform Rules shall apply to every contract of carriage of goods by rail for reward when the place of taking over of the goods and the place designated for delivery are situated in two different Member States, irrespective of the place of business and the nationality of the parties to the contract of carriage."
- **§2** "These Uniform Rules shall apply also to contracts of carriage of goods by rail for reward, when the place of taking over of the goods and the place designated for delivery are situated in two different States, of which at least one is a Member State and the parties to the contract agree that the contract is subject to these Uniform Rules."
- **§3** "When international carriage being the subject of a single contract includes carriage by road or inland waterway in internal traffic of a Member State as a supplement to transfrontier carriage by rail, these Uniform Rules shall apply."
- **§4** "When international carriage being the subject of a single contract of carriage includes carriage by sea or transfrontier carriage by inland waterway as a supplement to carriage by rail, these Uniform Rules shall apply if the carriage by sea or inland waterway is performed on services included in the list of services provided for in Article 24 § 1 of the Convention." That list is the "CIM list of maritime and inland waterway services" (COTIF Art. 24 §1 b).
- **§5** "These Uniform Rules shall not apply to carriage performed between stations situated on the territory of neighbouring States, when the infrastructure of these stations is managed by one or more infrastructure managers subject to only one of those States."
- **§6** A declaring state applies CIM "only on the condition a) that the place of taking over of the goods or the place designated for delivery, as well as the route designated in the contract of carriage, is situated on the specified infrastructure or b) that the specified infrastructure connects the infrastructure of two Member States and that it has been designated in the contract of carriage as a route for transit carriage."
- **§7** A reservation may be withdrawn by notification, effective one month later.
- **Art. 2:** CIM carriage "shall remain subject to the prescriptions of public law, in particular … dangerous goods … customs law and … the protection of animals."

## 3. CIM Arts. 6, 7, 8, 11, 12 (verbatim)

**Art. 6, Contract of carriage**
- §1 "By the contract of carriage, the carrier shall undertake to carry the goods for reward to the place of destination and to deliver them there to the consignee."
- §2 "The contract of carriage must be confirmed by a consignment note which accords with a uniform model. However, the absence, irregularity or loss of the consignment note shall not affect the existence or validity of the contract which shall remain subject to these Uniform Rules."
- §3 "The consignment note shall be signed by the consignor and the carrier. The signature can be replaced by a stamp, by an accounting machine entry or in any other appropriate manner."
- §4 "The carrier must certify the taking over of the goods on the duplicate of the consignment note in an appropriate manner and return the duplicate to the consignor."
- §5 "The consignment note shall not have effect as a bill of lading."
- §6 "A consignment note must be made out for each consignment. In the absence of a contrary agreement between the consignor and the carrier, a consignment note may not relate to more than one wagon load."
- §7 "In the case of carriage which takes place on the customs territory of the European Union or the territory on which the common transit procedure is applied, each consignment must be accompanied by a consignment note satisfying the requirements of Article 7."
- §8 "The international associations of carriers shall establish uniform model consignment notes in agreement with the customers' international associations and the bodies having competence for customs matters in the Member States as well as any intergovernmental regional economic integration organisation having competence to adopt its own customs legislation."
- §9 "The consignment note and its duplicate may be established in the form of electronic data registration which can be transformed into legible written symbols. The procedure used for the registration and treatment of data must be equivalent from the functional point of view, particularly so far as concerns the evidential value of the consignment note represented by those data."
- The words "functional equivalence" are not in Art. 6 itself. CIT's GLV-CIM uses them: "ensure the functional equivalence required by Article 6 § 9 CIM".

**Art. 7, Wording of the consignment note**

§1 "The consignment note must contain the following particulars:
- a) the place at which and the day on which it is made out;
- b) the name and address of the consignor;
- c) the name and address of the carrier who has concluded the contract of carriage;
- d) the name and address of the person to whom the goods have effectively been handed over if he is not the carrier referred to in letter c);
- e) the place and the day of taking over of the goods;
- f) the place of delivery;
- g) the name and address of the consignee;
- h) the description of the nature of the goods and the method of packing, and, in case of dangerous goods, the description provided for in the Regulation concerning the International Carriage of Dangerous Goods by Rail (RID);
- i) the number of packages and the special marks and numbers necessary for the identification of consignments in less than full wagon loads;
- j) the number of the wagon in the case of carriage of full wagon loads;
- k) the number of the railway vehicle running on its own wheels, if it is handed over for carriage as goods;
- l) in addition, in the case of intermodal transport units, the category, the number or other characteristics necessary for their identification;
- m) the gross mass or the quantity of the goods expressed in other ways;
- n) a detailed list of the documents which are required by customs or other administrative authorities and are attached to the consignment note or held at the disposal of the carrier at the offices of a duly designated authority or a body designated in the contract;
- o) the costs relating to carriage (the carriage charge, incidental costs, customs duties and other costs incurred from the conclusion of the contract until delivery) in so far as they must be paid by the consignee or any other statement that the costs are payable by the consignee;
- p) a statement that the carriage is subject, notwithstanding any clause to the contrary, to these Uniform Rules."

§2 "Where applicable the consignment note must also contain the following particulars (§2 runs a)–h), not to k):
- a) in the case of carriage by successive carriers, the carrier who must deliver the goods when he has consented to this entry in the consignment note;
- b) the costs which the consignor undertakes to pay;
- c) the amount of the cash on delivery charge;
- d) the declaration of the value of the goods and the amount representing the special interest in delivery;
- e) the agreed transit period;
- f) the agreed route;
- g) a list of the documents not mentioned in § 1, letter n) handed over to the carrier;
- h) the entries made by the consignor concerning the number and description of seals he has affixed to the wagon."

§3 "The parties to the contract may enter on the consignment note any other particulars they consider useful."

**Art. 8, Responsibility for particulars**
- §1: the consignor is responsible for the carrier's costs, loss or damage caused by "a) the entries made by the consignor in the consignment note being irregular, incorrect, incomplete or made elsewhere than in the allotted space, or b) the consignor omitting to make the entries prescribed by RID."
- §2 "If, at the request of the consignor, the carrier makes entries on the consignment note, he shall be deemed, unless the contrary is proved, to have done so on behalf of the consignor."
- §3 "If the consignment note does not contain the statement provided for in Article 7 § 1, letter p), the carrier shall be liable for all costs, loss or damage sustained through such omission by the person entitled." So the "this carriage is subject to CIM" line is load-bearing.

**Art. 11, Examination (brief)**
- §1: the carrier may examine at any time whether the consignment matches the consignor's entries. Where contents are examined, the person entitled is present where possible; otherwise "two independent witnesses".
- §2: the result is entered on the copy accompanying the goods and on the duplicate if the carrier still holds it.
- §3: where the consignor loads, he may require the carrier to examine "the condition of the goods and their packaging as well as the accuracy of statements … as to the number of packages, their marks and numbers as well as the gross mass". The carrier must do so "only if he has appropriate means", may charge for it, and "The result of the examination shall be entered on the consignment note."

**Art. 12, Evidential value**
- §1 "The consignment note shall be prima facie evidence of the conclusion and the conditions of the contract of carriage and the taking over of the goods by the carrier."
- §2 (carrier loaded): it is prima facie evidence of the condition of the goods and packaging as indicated, "or, in the absence of such indications, of their apparently good condition … and of the accuracy of the statements … concerning the number of packages, their marks and numbers as well as the gross mass".
- §3 (consignor loaded): it is such evidence "solely in the case where the carrier has examined them and recorded on the consignment note a result of his examination which tallies."
- §4 "However, the consignment note will not be prima facie evidence in a case where it bears a reasoned reservation. A reason for a reservation could be that the carrier does not have the appropriate means to examine whether the consignment corresponds to the entries in the consignment note."

Source for all of §3: https://otif.org/fileadmin/new/3-Reference-Text/3A-COTIF99/05_Appendix_B.pdf

## 4. The CIT CIM consignment note (GLV-CIM)

**Version.** "CIM Consignment Note Manual (GLV-CIM)", **Edition 1 July 2025** (Amendment No 7), "Applicable with effect from 1 January 2017".
- It states: "This document may be shared with the public."
- It is a recommendation: it "only binds members to the extent that members adopt it (opting-in principle)".
- EN PDF: https://www.cit-rail.org/secure-media/files/documentation/freight/glv-cim/glv-2024-07-01/glv-cim_en_2025-07-01_signed.pdf?cid=416057 (index: https://cit-rail.org/en/freight-traffic/manuals/?id=94810)

**Structure.**
- Appendix 1 covers the electronic note. Appendix 2 is the box-by-box explanatory notes. Appendix 4 has the paper rules, 4a the specimen CIM note, 4b the combined-transport note, and 5 the wagon list.
- "Wagon lists form an integral part of the consignment note."
- One note per wagon, with exceptions (block train, group of wagons, UTI) "set down in customer agreements".
- Languages: the note is printed and completed "in one or more languages of which one must be either English, or French or German", unless agreed otherwise.
- Boxes 1–30 are filled out by the consignor unless agreed otherwise.

**Pre-printed clause** to the left of box 30 (Appendix 4a): "Not withstanding any clause to the contrary, carriage of goods shall be subject to the CIM Uniform Rules. In addition, the carrier's General Terms and Conditions of Carriage shall apply." This is the Art. 7 §1 p) statement.

**Boxes (Appendix 2), with M/C/O status where the extraction was clear**

  - 12–13 EUR pallets and box-pallets; 16 other declarations; 24 limited-quantity dangerous goods over 8 t; 26 waste; 42 NCTS presentation.
- **8 (O)** consignor's reference.
- **9 (C) Documents attached:** UN/EDIFACT 1001 codes; container or vehicle packing certificate if there is a sea leg with dangerous goods.

## 5. The electronic consignment note (e-CIM)

**CIT definition** (GLV-CIM §0 and §6): "Electronic record of the data representing the consignment note". It restates Art. 6 §9.
- GLV-CIM §7: carrier and customer "set down the messages to be exchanged and the ways in which electronic consignment note data will be exchanged in a contract (see point 4.1 of the GTC-CIM)". This is the EDI contract.
- §9 allows a "mixed system" of paper, electronic and printout.

**GLV-CIM Appendix 1, point 1** (requirements, verbatim): "The procedures used to record and handle data must ensure the functional equivalence required by Article 6 § 9 CIM … the procedure must in particular ensure that:
- a) electronic documents are authenticated;
- b) data is kept secure and protected;
- c) electronic documents can be transformed into legible written symbols and printed;
- d) amendments and additions to an electronic consignment note are recorded and earlier data retained;
- e) data is conserved in accordance with the rules on timescales laid down in the CIM Uniform Rules, the provisions of national law and the terms of the EDI contract;
- f) data on dangerous goods consignments is available at all times and unrestrictedly to carriers …"

Further Appendix 1 rules:
- **When messages count:** "EDI messages only have legal effect when the IT system of the EDI recipient receives the messages."
- **Who may change the record:** "The right to process the electronic consignment note belongs to the carrier who has custody of the goods."
- **Access:** "only allowed to participants who have concluded an EDI contract and who also take part in the contract of carriage". Rights are read, write or amend. "If the consignor changes the consignee, the original consignee is to lose his rights of access."
- **Printouts** must carry "'Printout of the electronic consignment note – YYYY-MM-DD (date of printing) – produced by … (carrier)'", and the system "must record the production of every printout".
- **Mixed media** "are to have the same evidential value".
- **Data groups** are numbered like the paper boxes (1–99). Message specifications are at http://www.raildata.coop/

**Technical specification** (the e-CIM/SMGS spec, "Edition 1 July 2019", CIT + OSJD): https://cit-rail.org/secure-media/files/e-fb_cim-smgs_en_2019-07-01_signe.pdf?cid=364515
- Schema "CIM_SMGS_ECN_1.4.xsd" (RailData), mapped to UN/EDIFACT **IFTMIN 97A** and used in RailData's **ORFEUS** central system.
- Signatures: the signature "must allow: 1. the parties to the contract to be identified; 2. the consent of the parties … to be confirmed … The type of electronic signature and the level of security … are to be defined by the national law applicable."
- Integrity: "The original data must remain (legible) in the system"; "IT systems must create an audit trail for all additions and alterations".
- Evidence: "No party may assert before a court or tribunal that the electronic data exchanged is not of itself adequate evidence of the contract of carriage."

**No separate protocol is needed.** CIM Art. 6 §9 has allowed the electronic note directly since 2006.
- By contrast, road needs the "Additional Protocol to the CMR concerning the Electronic Consignment Note" (Geneva, 20 Feb 2008, in force 5 June 2011, 42 parties): https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XI-B-11-b&chapter=11&clang=_en
- OTIF's 2025–26 amendments (§1 above) aim to make CIM "fully and expressly" provide for electronic documents. They are not in force.

**EU eFTI, Regulation (EU) 2020/1056.**
- It covers road, rail, inland waterway and air (Commission page).
- Art. 2(1) applies to *regulatory* information requirements only. These include Art. 6(1) of Regulation No 11, Art. 3 of Directive 92/106/EEC (combined transport), Regulation 1013/2006 (waste), and "Chapter 5.4 of Part 5 of the … (RID)", plus listed delegated or implementing acts and national law in Annex I.
- CIM, the private-law contract document, is not among them. eFTI does not make an e-CIM valid; Art. 6 §9 does that.
- Art. 5(1): authorities "shall accept regulatory information made available electronically … As from 30 months after the date of entry into force of the first of the delegated and implementing acts referred to in Articles 7 and 8".
- Art. 18: entry into force on the 20th day after publication; "It shall apply from 21 August 2024".
- Art. 9(1)(g): operation logs, and "if an operation involves modifying or erasing an existing data element, the original data element shall be preserved".
- There is no obligation on operators. Art. 16 review by 21 Feb 2029 is to assess one.
- Text as adopted: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2020/1056/body/adopted (EUR-Lex blocked automated access).
- Commission page (https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/logistics-and-multimodal-transport/efti-regulation_en):
  - acts: Implementing Reg. 2024/1942 (5 Jul 2024), Delegated Reg. 2024/2024 (26 Jul 2024, the data set), Delegated Reg. 2024/2025 (15 Jul 2024), Implementing Reg. 2025/2243 (6 Nov 2025, platform requirements)
  - authorities "may start accepting" from January 2026
  - full application on **9 July 2027**.

## 6. CIM/SMGS and SMGS

**The common note.** GLV-CIM/SMGS, "Edition 12th December 2025", "Applicable with effect from 1 January 2019", issued by CIT and OSJD; a recommendation for CIT members: https://www.cit-rail.org/secure-media/files/documentation/freight/glv-cimsmgs/glv_cim-smgs_en_2025-12-12.pdf?cid=452017
- **Legal basis:** "Art. 6 § 8 CIM and art. 13 SMGS form the legal basis for the CIM/SMGS consignment note."
- **Purpose:** "an alternative to the classic system of consignment with rewriting of a SMGS consignment note to a CIM consignment note … at the reconsignment point." It covers two successive contracts, CIM then SMGS or the reverse.
- **CIM side:** it applies only if agreed between customer and carrier and between the carriers.
- **Languages:** Russian plus English, French or German, and Chinese for China.
- **Who uses it:** Appendix 1 lists about 45 CIT carriers (e.g. DB Cargo, PKP, RCA, SNCF, SBB, UZ, Georgian Railway, RAI Iran). SMGS participants: Azerbaijan, Belarus, Bulgaria, China, Estonia, Georgia, Hungary, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Latvia, Lithuania, Moldova, Mongolia, Poland, Russia, Slovakia, Turkmenistan, Ukraine. Each has defined traffic axes. https://www.cit-rail.org/media/files/documentation/freight/glv-cimsmgs/glv_cim-smgs_en_2025-09-01_app_1_xvfGgyQ.pdf
- **In practice (CIT):** the note is used for east–west Eurasian traffic and is recognised as a customs transit document and a documentary-credit document. https://uncitral.un.org/sites/uncitral.un.org/files/media-documents/uncitral/en/Webinardematerializationnegotiabletransportdocuments/presentations/erik_evtimov.pdf

**SMGS itself.** "Applicable from 1 November 1951 with amendments and supplements as of 1 July 2026": https://en.osjd.org/en/8910/page/106077?id=2990
- **26 parties:** Albania, Azerbaijan, Afghanistan, Belarus, Bulgaria, China, Estonia, Georgia, Hungary, Iran, Kazakhstan, DPR Korea, Kyrgyzstan, Lao PDR, Latvia, Lithuania, Moldova, Mongolia, Poland, Russia, Slovakia, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Ukraine, Uzbekistan, Vietnam.
- Art. 13: "The goods may be carried with the CIM/SMGS Consignment note … (See Annex 6 to this Agreement)."
- Art. 14 §7: electronic documents "may be used, subject to approval by the carriers participating in the carriage".
- Art. 15 §1 lists 17 required particulars. §4: "The consignment note may be completed as a paper or an electronic consignment note."
- OSJD membership (https://en.osjd.org/en/9182) is not the same as being an SMGS party. The Republic of Korea, Cuba and the Czech Republic are OSJD members but not SMGS parties.
- **No African state** is an SMGS party or an OSJD member.

## 7. East Africa (from the two sub-agents' primary downloads; key quotes re-checked by me)

**No CIM/SMGS-style international rail regime applies.** Each state has its own railway statute and its railway's tariff-book conditions. The corridor treaties regulate the interface between carriers, not the shipper's contract.

**Kenya**
- **Cap. 397 has been repealed, per KRC itself.** KRC Tariff Notice No. 4 of 2026 (for traffic booked on or after 1 June 2026): "Following the repeal of the Kenya Railways Corporation Act (Cap. 397) and the commencement of the GOE Act, 2025…" https://krc.co.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Kenya-Railways-Tariff-Book.pdf
  - The GOE Act 2025 is Act No. 25 of 2025.
  - A High Court stay (Feb 2026) was reported; I could not confirm its outcome.
  - A draft Railways Bill 2024 would carry the carriage rules over to any "rail carrier": https://krc.co.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/The-Railways-Bill.pdf. Its status is unknown.
- **The same Tariff Book governs SGR and MGR freight.** Its definition: "'CONSIGNMENT NOTE' means contractual and accounting documents … lodged /created electronically to facilitate receipt, invoicing, transportation and delivery of cargo".
- **Appendix II, "Standard Conditions of Carriage of Goods":**
  - cl.7: a note "in the prescribed form and signed by the consignor".
  - cl.8: "The consignment note must show clearly the name of the station or port of tender of the goods, the date month and year of tender, a full and accurate description of the goods and type of packing, the marks and number of the packages, the gross weight of the goods the name and address of the consignor and consignee, the destination station or port, how the carriage charges are payable and must state full particulars of all Customs or other documents which may be required to accompany the consignment."
  - cl.9: the declared value is the maximum liability, and "Kshs. 20,000/=" applies where no value is declared.
  - cl.22: notice of claim within 7 days; claim within 6 months.
  - The second cl.23 (the notice has two clauses numbered 23): suit within 1 year.
  - cl.32: "All contracts for carriage of goods by the Company shall be governed by either the Laws of Kenya or Uganda."
- **Old Cap. 397 Part VII** (railway liable unless it proves listed causes; 6-month claim; 12-month suit) is quoted only from a secondary mirror. Kenya Law blocked access. It is now repealed anyway.
- **Electronic notes:** KICA ss.83G, 83J and 83O recognise electronic writing, contracts and advanced e-signatures: https://ca.go.ke/sites/default/files/CA/Statutes%20and%20Regulations/Kenya-Information-and-Communication-Act-1998.pdf. The Evidence Act s.106B covers electronic evidence.

**Uganda: URC Act Cap. 331** (ULII text as at 31.12.2000, with Act 17 of 2023 not yet applied): https://media.ulii.org/media/legislation/17847/source_file/2ac276819de5fbfd/1992-13.pdf
- s.1(h): a consignment is goods "entered on an official consignment note".
- s.58(1): the corporation is not liable "unless it is proved that the loss … arose out of negligence". This is the reverse of Kenya's old rule.
- s.61: liability is capped at the declared value.
- s.51: non-delivery claim within 3 months; notice within 7 days.
- s.52(b): suit within 12 months.
- s.67: conditions are published in a "tariff notice". URC's conditions were not found.
- The Uganda SGR is under construction (contract Oct 2024).

**Northern Corridor (NCTTA 2007**, in force 6 Dec 2012). Parties: Burundi, DR Congo, Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda, plus South Sudan (accession approved 2012). Ethiopia is not a party. https://www.ttcanc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/English-Bk.pdf
- **Protocol No. 5, "Transport of Goods by Rail":** carriers at border stations "hand over wagons, goods and documents". It deals with liability between carriers and to third persons (Arts. 11–13: "regulated by agreement between the rail carriers").
- **What Protocol 5 lacks:** no shipper contract regime, no consignment note, no cargo limits, no applicable-law rule.
- **Protocol No. 4, Art. 7:** "The Contracting Parties agree to accept the Standard Rail Waybill shown in Appendix 2 to this Protocol for the inter-state and transit transport of goods by rail." Appendix 2 is not reproduced in the published PDFs.
- **Main Agreement:** the parties "agree to implement and maintain a unified liability regime for their rail carriers under a single document and contract". This is a commitment only.

**Central Corridor (CCTTFA 2006).** Burundi, DR Congo, Rwanda, Tanzania, Uganda; Malawi and Zambia acceded in 2023.
- Protocol 4, Art. 7 (I verified it from the scan): "The Corridor Member States agree to accept the Standard Rail Waybill in use in each other respective territories for the inter-state and transit transport of goods by rail."
- Protocol 5 (rail) deals only with carriers. https://centralcorridor-ttfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/TTFA-ENGLISH-PROTOCOLS-1-5-AUGUST-2014-KIGALI-RWANDA.pdf

**Tanzania: Railways Act 2017 (Act 10/2017, Cap. 170)** (TanzLII as at 14.07.2023): https://media.tanzlii.org/media/legislation/325008/source_file/102bfe86d1eedb4c/2017-10.pdf
- s.2(2): "This Act shall not apply to the undertaking of the Tanzania Zambia Railway Authority."
- The document is a "document of receipt" (s.52(4)); the Act never uses "consignment note".
- s.38: the Corporation is liable unless it proves listed causes.
- s.41: specified valuables are capped at "two million shillings" unless declared.
- Conditions are set in the Tariff Book (s.52).
- No claims time limit found.
- LATRA regulates rail tariffs and licences.
- SGR freight began in June 2025; no published SGR conditions were found.

**TAZARA.** Tanzania-Zambia Railway Act: Cap. 143 (Tanzania, Act 4/1995) and Cap. 454 (Zambia, Act 10/1995), giving effect to the 1993 Agreement.
- A consignment is goods sent "on one consignment note".
- s.40: the Authority is liable unless it proves listed causes.
- s.84: written claim "within ninety days of the date on which such goods were contracted to be delivered" (verified in both Acts).
- Sources: https://commons.laws.africa/akn/tz/act/1995/4/eng@2002-07-31.pdf and https://commons.laws.africa/akn/zm/act/1995/10/eng@1996-12-31.pdf

**Ethiopia–Djibouti.**
- The bilateral SGR agreement (signed 14 July 2012) was ratified by Proclamation 1014/2017. The treaty text is not annexed.
- Proclamation 1048/2017 (railway administration): tariffs and "conditions" must be approved by the Ministry.
- **The carriage regime is Proclamation 547/2007** (carriage of goods by land). It is CMR-style, and "VEHICLE … includes … rail wagons" (Art. 2(8)).
  - Consignment note in three originals; Art. 6 particulars.
  - Limit SDR 835 per package or SDR 2.5/kg; suit within 2 years.
  - This was read by the sub-agent from a noisy scan. Check it against a clean copy.
- Proclamation 548/2007 (multimodal) covers rail legs.
- https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2007/pr_547_2007.pdf

## 8. CIM liability headlines (OTIF text)

- **Art. 30 §2:** "Compensation shall not exceed 17 units of account per kilogramme of gross mass short."
  - COTIF Art. 9 §1: "The unit of account … shall be the Special Drawing Right as defined by the International Monetary Fund."
  - Art. 30 §4: carriage charge, duties paid and other sums are refunded in addition.
- **Art. 32:** damage is compensated by loss in value, capped at the loss amount.
- **Art. 33 §1:** where the transit period is exceeded, compensation is "not exceeding four times the carriage charge".
- **Art. 34:** "The consignor and the carrier may agree that the consignor shall declare in the consignment note a value for the goods exceeding the limit provided for in Article 30 § 2. In such a case the amount declared shall be substituted for that limit."
- **Art. 35:** by agreement, the consignor may declare "by entering an amount in figures in the consignment note, a special interest in delivery". Further proved loss may then be claimed "up to the amount declared".
- **Art. 36:** the limits do not apply in cases of intent or recklessness "with knowledge that such loss or damage would probably result".
- **Art. 47:** "Acceptance of the goods by the person entitled shall extinguish all rights of action" for partial loss, damage or delay. The exceptions are:
  - loss or damage ascertained before acceptance
  - hidden damage, where ascertainment is asked "not later than seven days after the acceptance"
  - delay, where rights are asserted "within sixty days"
  - intent or recklessness.
- **Art. 48 §1:** "The period of limitation for an action arising from the contract of carriage shall be one year." It is two years for cash on delivery, sale proceeds, intent or recklessness, and reconsignment.
  - §2: it runs for total loss "from the thirtieth day after expiry of the transit period", and for partial loss, damage or delay "from the day when delivery took place".
  - §3: it is suspended by a written claim (Art. 43).
