### 1.2 How the regime is chosen
**MC99 Art. 1(2):** "international carriage means any carriage in which, according to the agreement between the parties, the place of departure and the place of destination … are situated either within the territories of two States Parties, or within the territory of a single State Party if there is an agreed stopping place within the territory of another State, even if that State is not a State Party."
- Two points inside one State Party with no agreed stop abroad is "not international carriage" under MC99.

**Art. 1(3):** successive carriers count as "one undivided carriage if it has been regarded by the parties as a single operation". A leg performed entirely inside one state does not lose its international character.

**Art. 55:** MC99 "shall prevail over any rules which apply to international carriage by air: 1. between States Parties to this Convention by virtue of those States commonly being Party to" Warsaw, Hague, Guadalajara, Guatemala City, or "Additional Protocol Nos. 1 to 3 and Montreal Protocol No. 4".

**Warsaw/Hague Art. 1(2)** (Hague text, UK Carriage by Air Act 1961 as enacted, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Eliz2/9-10/27/pdfs/ukpga_19610027_en.pdf): the same test, but "within the territories of two High Contracting Parties".

**Rule for a lane (synthesis from these articles; common practice, but not verified against case law in every forum):**
1. Read the places of departure and destination from the contract: the AWB, or the HAWB for a house shipment. Not the flight path.
2. Both ends MC99 → **MC99**.
3. Otherwise, take the newest Warsaw-system instrument both ends share:
   - both MAP4 → **Warsaw/Hague/MAP4**
   - else both Hague → **Warsaw/Hague**
   - else both Warsaw → **Warsaw 1929**
4. Nothing in common → **no convention**: the contract (the carrier's conditions) plus national law, which law being chosen by the court's conflict rules.
5. A round trip from one state with an agreed stop abroad → MC99 or Warsaw "single State Party" rule. Domestic carriage → the national row (§8).

- **Uganda example:** *Katumba v Kenya Airways* applied "the Warsaw Convention and not the Hague amendment". At that time Uganda was Warsaw-only and Kenya was a Hague party. Source: ULII search summary only, not verified (from fork §8).
- **Ethiopia example:** Ethiopia is Warsaw-only for Hague purposes but ratified MAP4 (and AP1/AP2) on 14 Jul 1987. I believe MAP4 Art. XVII gives that ratification the effect of accession to Warsaw as amended by Hague and MAP4, but I did **not verify** it; the MAP4 final clauses were not read.

## 2. Liability for cargo

### 2.1 MC99 (text: IATA copy above)
- **Strict liability, Art. 18(1):** "upon condition only that the event which caused the damage so sustained took place during the carriage by air".
  - Art. 18(3): that period "comprises the period during which the cargo is in the charge of the carrier".
- **The only cargo defences, Art. 18(2):** "if and to the extent it proves" (a) "inherent defect, quality or vice of that cargo"; (b) "defective packing … performed by a person other than the carrier"; (c) war or armed conflict; (d) "an act of public authority carried out in connection with the entry, exit or transit of the cargo".
  - Art. 20 adds exoneration for the claimant's own negligence.
  - Live animals and perishables mostly meet (a) and (b) in practice (synthesis).
- **Delay, Art. 19:** liable unless the carrier "took all measures that could reasonably be required to avoid the damage or that it was impossible … to take such measures".
- **Limit, Art. 22(3):** "17 Special Drawing Rights per kilogramme, unless the consignor has made, at the time when the package was handed over to the carrier, a special declaration of interest in delivery at destination and has paid a supplementary sum if the case so requires". The declared sum then caps liability unless the carrier proves it exceeds "the consignor's actual interest".
  - Art. 22(4): the weight counted is "only the total weight of the package or packages concerned", unless the loss affects the value of other packages on the same AWB.
- **The limit cannot be broken for cargo.** Art. 22(5), the intent/recklessness exception, covers only "paragraphs 1 and 2" (passengers and baggage), not paragraph 3. Art. 30(3) gives servants and agents the same protection: "Save in respect of the carriage of cargo".
  - Art. 25: the carrier may agree higher limits or none.
  - Art. 26: lower limits are void.
  - Art. 22(6): costs and interest may be awarded on top.
- **Revised cargo limit (Art. 24 reviews):**

The MC99 list calls it "the fourth review". |

- **Conversion, Art. 23(1):** SDR to national currency "at the date of the judgement".
  - Non-IMF states may use "250 monetary units per kilogramme" in gold (Art. 23(2)).
- **Notice of complaint, Art. 31:**
  - Damage: "forthwith after the discovery … and, at the latest, within … fourteen days from the date of receipt in the case of cargo".
  - Delay: "within twenty-one days from the date on which the … cargo have been placed at his or her disposal".
  - The complaint must be "in writing" (31(3)). Without it, "no action shall lie … save in the case of fraud" (31(4)).
  - Receipt without complaint is prima facie good delivery (31(1)).
  - Art. 31 sets no notice period for total loss.
  - Art. 52: "days" means "calendar days".
- **Non-arrival, Art. 13(3):** the consignee can sue once the cargo "has not arrived at the expiration of seven days after the date on which it ought to have arrived".
- **Time bar, Art. 35:** the right "shall be extinguished if an action is not brought within a period of two years, reckoned from the date of arrival at the destination, or from the date on which the aircraft ought to have arrived, or from the date on which the carriage stopped". The court's own law sets the counting method.
- **Jurisdiction, Art. 33(1):** in a State Party, at the claimant's option: the carrier's domicile, its principal place of business, its place of business "through which the contract has been made", or "the place of destination".
  - Art. 34: cargo arbitration is allowed if "in writing", seated in an Art. 33 jurisdiction, and applying MC99.
  - Art. 46: an extra forum at the actual carrier's domicile.
  - Art. 49: clauses changing the law or the forum before the damage are void.

### 2.2 Routes that are not MC99
- **Warsaw 1929.** English text: UK Carriage by Air Act 1932 as enacted, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo5/22-23/36/pdfs/ukpga_19320036_en.pdf
  - Art. 22(2): "250 francs per kilogram", unless value was declared. A franc is "65½ milligrams gold of millesimal fineness nine hundred".
  - Art. 20(1) defence: "all necessary measures". Art. 20(2) adds, for goods, "negligent pilotage or negligence in the handling of the aircraft or in navigation".
  - Art. 25: no limits for "wilful misconduct".
  - Art. 26(2): complaint "within … seven days from the date of receipt in the case of goods"; delay "within fourteen days".
  - Art. 29: two years.
- **Warsaw/Hague.** Sources: UK 1961 Act as enacted (above), and Kenya Carriage by Air Act First Schedule, https://infotradekenya.go.ke/media/Carriage%20by%20Air%20Act%202%20of%201993.pdf
  - Art. 22(2)(a): "two hundred and fifty francs per kilogramme".
  - Art. 25: limits fall for intent, or "recklessly and with knowledge that damage would probably result". This applies to cargo.
  - Art. 26(2): 14 days for damage, 21 days for delay. Art. 29: two years.
- **Warsaw/Hague/MAP4.** Source: UK 1961 Act Schedule 1A, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Eliz2/9-10/27/schedule/1A
  - Art. 22(2)(a): "17 Special Drawing Rights per kilogramme".
  - Art. 18(3) gives the same four defences as MC99, but "resulted solely from".
  - Art. 24(2) makes the cargo limit unbreakable: "Such limits of liability constitute maximum limits and may not be exceeded whatever the circumstances which give rise to the liability". Art. 25 is limited to "passengers and baggage".
  - Art. 26(2): 14 days for damage, 21 days for delay.
- **Gold-franc conversion** (e.g. the "US$20/kg" or ~16.58 SDR equivalences): **not verified**. Kenya's own conversion is in §8.

### 2.3 The airline's own terms where no convention applies
IATA Res. 600i, reprinted in the Res. 672 annex (https://www.iata.org/contentassets/4bc75639b37641ba88f2e81e5516a020/multilateral-resolution-672.docx):
- Para 4: "For carriage to which the Montreal Convention does not apply, Carrier's liability limitation … shall be 26 SDRs per kilogram unless a greater … limit is provided in any applicable Convention".

## 3. The air waybill

### 3.1 What MC99 requires on it
- **Art. 4:** "an air waybill shall be delivered". (2) "Any other means which preserves a record of the carriage to be performed may be substituted". If so, the carrier must, on request, deliver "a cargo receipt permitting identification of the consignment and access to the information".
- **Art. 5, the only mandatory particulars:** "(a) an indication of the places of departure and destination; (b) if the places of departure and destination are within the territory of a single State Party, one or more agreed stopping places being within the territory of another State, an indication of at least one such stopping place; and (c) an indication of the weight of the consignment."
  - MC99 does not require shipper or consignee names.
- **Art. 6:** the consignor "may be required … to deliver a document indicating the nature of the cargo" for customs and police. This creates no carrier duty. Art. 16 makes the consignor liable for missing customs documents; the carrier "is under no obligation to enquire into the correctness" of them.
- **Art. 7, three originals made out by the consignor:**
  - first "for the carrier", signed by the consignor
  - second "for the consignee", signed by both
  - third signed by the carrier and handed to the consignor "after the cargo has been accepted"
  - Signatures "may be printed or stamped". If the carrier makes out the AWB at the consignor's request, it is "deemed … to have done so on behalf of the consignor".
- **Art. 8:** separate AWBs per package can be required; separate cargo receipts can be demanded.
- **Art. 9:** "Non-compliance with the provisions of Articles 4 to 8 shall not affect the existence or the validity of the contract of carriage, which shall, nonetheless, be subject to the rules of this Convention including those relating to limitation of liability."
- **Art. 10, responsibility for particulars:** the consignor is responsible for particulars "inserted by it or on its behalf". This "shall also apply where the person acting on behalf of the consignor is also the agent of the carrier", which covers an IATA agent filling in the AWB. The consignor must indemnify the carrier; the carrier is responsible for what it inserts in the cargo receipt or record.
- **Art. 11, evidentiary value:**
  - The AWB or cargo receipt is prima facie evidence of the contract, of acceptance of the cargo and of the conditions.
  - Statements of "weight, dimensions and packing … as well as … the number of packages" are prima facie evidence.
  - "quantity, volume and condition" are not evidence against the carrier unless checked "in the presence of the consignor", or they concern apparent condition.
- **Art. 12, disposition:** if the carrier obeys disposition instructions without "requiring the production of the part of the air waybill … delivered to" the consignor, it is liable to whoever lawfully holds that part. The consignor's original therefore matters.
- **Art. 15(2):** Arts. 12–14 can be varied only by express provision in the AWB.

### 3.2 Warsaw and Hague particulars, and what happens when they are missing
- **Hague Art. 8** (UK 1961 Act as enacted) requires: (a) places of departure and destination; (b) the agreed stopping place, where both are in one state; and "(c) a notice to the consignor to the effect that, if the carriage involves an ultimate destination or stop in a country other than the country of departure, the Warsaw Convention may be applicable and that the Convention governs and in most cases limits the liability of carriers in respect of loss of or damage to cargo."
- **Hague Art. 9:** "If, with the consent of the carrier, cargo is loaded on board the aircraft without an air waybill having been made out, or if the air waybill does not include the notice required by Article 8, paragraph (c), the carrier shall not be entitled to avail himself of the provisions of Article 22, paragraph (2)." In other words, **no limit**.
- **Warsaw 1929 Art. 8:** 17 particulars (a)–(q) (UK 1932 Act):
  - place and date of execution; places of departure and destination; agreed stopping places
  - names and addresses of consignor, first carrier, and consignee "if the case so requires"
  - nature of goods; number of packages, packing and marks; weight, quantity, volume or dimensions
  - apparent condition; freight; cash-on-delivery price; declared value
  - number of parts; accompanying documents; time and route agreed
  - a statement that carriage is "subject to the rules relating to liability established by this Convention"
- **Warsaw 1929 Art. 9:** if the AWB "does not contain all the particulars set out in Article 8 (a) to (i) inclusive and (q), the carrier shall not be entitled to avail himself of the provisions of this Convention which exclude or limit his liability."
- **MAP4** (Schedule 1A):
  - Art. 8 = the same three particulars as MC99, including weight.
  - Art. 9: non-compliance does not affect validity or limits.
  - Art. 5(2) allows "any other means which would preserve a record" **"with the consent of the consignor"**. MC99 has no such consent condition.

## 4. The electronic air waybill (fork §4; IATA primary sources)
- **The law allows it.** MC99 Art. 4(2), quoted above, permits a record instead of a paper AWB.
- **IATA ties e-AWB to the treaty.** "Feasible trade lane is defined as such when country of origin and country of destination ratified the same treaty - either the Montreal Protocol No. 4 of 1975 (MP4) or the Montreal Convention of 1999 (MC99)". Elsewhere "the use of paper AWB is still required". Source: https://www.iata.org/contentassets/4bc75639b37641ba88f2e81e5516a020/e-awb-implementation-playbook.pdf
- **Res. 672, the Multilateral e-AWB Agreement (MeA).**
  - Signing: IATA signs for the airlines listed in Attachment B; each forwarder signs once with IATA. "Freight Forwarder" is "merely a placeholder for the specific shipper/agent/forwarder or other authorized EDI counterparty".
  - Joining is free, "enables, but does not oblige", and is for international shipments only. Source: https://www.iata.org/contentassets/4bc75639b37641ba88f2e81e5516a020/mea-faq.pdf
  - Nothing runs until the airline sends an Activation Notice for named airports.
  - The Shipment Record has "the legal effect of a duly executed paper air waybill to the full extent permitted by national law". On conflict, "the Electronic Communication shall be governing".
  - The Cargo Receipt (600g) is "deemed a full substitute for a Carrier air waybill".
  - The carrier keeps the record "unaltered and secured for a minimum of two years" and printable.
- **Default since 1 January 2019.** "Effective 1 January 2019, the electronic Air Waybill (e-AWB) will become the default contract of carriage for all air cargo shipments on enabled trade lanes". Source: https://www.iata.org/contentassets/4bc75639b37641ba88f2e81e5516a020/eawb-default.pdf
- **Message flow** (e-AWB Global SOP: https://www.iata.org/contentassets/4bc75639b37641ba88f2e81e5516a020/iata_eawb_global_sop.pdf):
  1. The forwarder sends the master data as XFWB (Cargo-IMP FWB), plus XFZB/FHL for houses.
  2. It flags EAW (no accompanying documents or pouch) or EAP (documents or pouch accompany).
  3. The airline replies XFNM/FMA (accepted) or FNA (errors), and sets ECC ("A paper AWB is not required") or ECP (print one).
  4. Freight on hand: "(X)FSU-FOH" plus a Warehouse Receipt. "For e-AWB under the MeA, the cargo contract is deemed concluded at the time the status message (X)FSU-FOH is sent".
  5. Ready for carriage: "(X)FSU-RCS" plus the Cargo Receipt. The shipment is now "under the liability of the carrier".
- **Shipper's Delivery Note.** Its minimum content is shipper, weight, pieces, date/time/place received and shipment ID. Once checked and countersigned it "shall serve as a Warehouse Receipt" (Res. 672 Art. 2.15).
- **Adoption.** IATA says e-AWB is "now used for more than 2 out of 3 shipments" (undated): https://www.iata.org/en/programs/cargo/e/one-record/. The last dated figure is 55.9% in September 2018. No 2025 or 2026 figure was found.
- **ONE Record in 2026.**
  - The Cargo Services Conference (CSC) endorsed it "as the preferred standard for data sharing … effective 1 January 2026". Source: https://www.iata.org/en/iata-repository/pressroom/fact-sheets/fact-sheet-one-record/
  - Release 2026-07 (12 Aug 2026) is Ontology 3.3.0 and API 2.3.0, MIT-licensed. From 1 Jan 2026 Cargo-XML is "in containment, meaning no new message or versions". Source: https://github.com/IATA-Cargo/ONE-Record/releases/tag/2026-07
  - The data model has a `Waybill` with `waybillType` House/Direct/Master and `masterWaybill`/`houseWaybills` links. Access Delegations can expire.
- **AWB number.** "an AWB number is made of 11-digits. The 11th is a check digit (unweighted Modulus 7)", format "000-00000000". Source: https://dgautocheck.iata.org/help/AirWaybillnumbervalidation.html
  - Australian Border Force: "The seven-digit serial number is simply divided by 7 and the resulting remainder is the check digit", example 1234567 → remainder 5 → 12345675. Source: https://www.abf.gov.au/sdg-subsite/Pages/Check-Digit-Algorithms/Air-Waybill-Number-Validation.aspx
  - The check digit is always 0–6. IATA's public code search does not cover 3-digit prefixes.
- **Public verification.** Airlines track by master AWB number only. No public registry confirms that an AWB was issued; this absence is itself **not verified**. IATA's Matchmaker list of e-AWB agreement participants returned 403.

## 5. House and master air waybills
- **Contracting carrier, MC99 Art. 39.** Chapter V applies when a person "as a principal makes a contract of carriage governed by this Convention with … consignor … and another person (… 'the actual carrier') performs, by virtue of authority from the contracting carrier, the whole or part of the carriage". "Such authority shall be presumed".
  - Art. 40: the contracting carrier is liable for the whole carriage, the actual carrier "solely for the carriage which it performs".
  - Art. 41(1): the actual carrier's acts are deemed the contracting carrier's.
  - Art. 41(2): "any special declaration of interest … shall not affect the actual carrier unless agreed to by it".
  - Art. 42: complaints go to either carrier, but disposition instructions under Art. 12 "shall only be effective if addressed to the contracting carrier".
  - Art. 44: damages aggregated up to the higher limit. Art. 45: sue either or both. Art. 46: extra forum. Art. 47: lower limits void.
- **Is a forwarder that issues a house AWB (HAWB) a contracting carrier?** The test is whether it contracts "as a principal" (Art. 39). That depends on the HAWB and its terms, not on the label (synthesis).
  - **Signals that it can be a carrier:**
    - US law: an "indirect cargo air carrier is any U.S. citizen who undertakes to engage indirectly in air transportation of property and uses … the services of an air carrier" (14 CFR 296.3, 2024 ed.: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title14-vol4/xml/CFR-2024-title14-vol4-part296.xml).
    - EU ICS2 calls the HAWB issuer's relation a "transport contract" (IA 2015/2447 Art. 184(3), fork §7).
  - **Case law** (e.g. *Fujitsu v Bax Global* [2005] EWHC 2289 (Comm)): **not verified**. BAILII blocks bots and no search was left.
- **The structure (synthesis):** on the master AWB (MAWB) the forwarder is the consignor and its destination agent the consignee. The real shipper and consignee appear only on the HAWB. A house shipper's claim lies against the forwarder as contracting carrier, and against the airline as actual carrier if the HAWB contract is governed by MC99.
- **FIATA** (fork §4/5; https://fiata.org/resources):
  - FIATA has **no house air waybill standard**. Its documents are FCR, FCT, FWR, the FBL (negotiable), the "Non-negotiable FIATA Multimodal Transport Waybill (FWB)" and the eFBL.
  - The FIATA FWB also covers single-mode carriage ("performed by only one mode of transport"). Its limit is 8.33 SDR/kg without sea, with a network clause where a convention "would have provided another limit" (clause 9.6(a)), which brings in MC99 for the air stage.
  - "FWB" means two different things: FIATA's waybill and IATA's FWB message.
  - IATA–FIATA "Air Cargo Programmes" exist only in Latin America, Canada and Europe. Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda sit under the IATA Cargo Agency Programme (Res. 801). The global IFACP's status is **not verified**.

## 6. Road legs tied to air
- **MC99 Art. 18(4):** "The period of the carriage by air does not extend to any carriage by land … performed outside an airport. If, however, such carriage takes place in the performance of a contract for carriage by air, for the purpose of loading, delivery or transshipment, any damage is presumed, subject to proof to the contrary, to have been the result of an event which took place during the carriage by air. If a carrier, without the consent of the consignor, substitutes carriage by another mode of transport for the whole or part of a carriage intended by the agreement between the parties to be carriage by air, such carriage by another mode of transport is deemed to be within the period of carriage by air."
- **The substitution sentence is MC99-only.** Warsaw 1929 Art. 18(3), Hague Art. 18(3) and MAP4 Art. 18(5) keep only the presumption, with no substitution rule (texts in §2.2; confirmed for MAP4).
- **Combined carriage, MC99 Art. 38:** MC99 applies "only to the carriage by air", subject to Art. 18(4). Parties may put other-mode conditions in the air document.
- **Airlines pre-authorise road.** Res. 600i notice: "ALL GOODS MAY BE CARRIED BY ANY OTHER MEANS INCLUDING ROAD OR ANY OTHER CARRIER UNLESS SPECIFIC CONTRARY INSTRUCTIONS ARE GIVEN BY THE SHIPPER". Para 9: "Carrier may use alternative carriers, aircraft or modes of transport without notice". Whether this counts as the "consent" that disapplies the substitution rule is a **lawyer question**.
- **Road feeder services.** That they run under an airline flight number is industry practice and **not verified** from a primary source.
  - EU ICS2 acknowledges air cargo moving by other modes: "Where air cargo is transported on modes of transport other than air, the other mode of transport shall be declared" (DA Annex B, fork §7).
- **CMR, if a leg crosses a border.** Art. 1(1): it applies "to every contract for the carriage of goods by road in vehicles for reward, when the place of taking over … and the place designated for delivery … are situated in two different countries, of which at least one is a Contracting country". Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1965/37/schedule
  - Art. 2 (roll-on) needs the loaded vehicle itself to go by air, which a feeder truck does not.
  - No East African state is a CMR party (project catalogue `04_platform/backend/src/foundation/carriage-law/catalogue.ts`, UN Treaty Collection, checked 2026-10-28).
  - *Quantum v Plane Trucking* [2002] EWCA Civ 350 (CMR applied to a road feeder leg under an air waybill): **not verified**; BAILII blocked.
- **What governs a loss on our truck (synthesis):**
  1. **We carry for the shipper or consignee under our own contract** (farm → terminal; terminal → farm), separate from the air contract. The air period has not begun or has ended (Art. 18(3): "in the charge of the carrier").
     - Our road law applies: national law, the CMR if a CMR state is at one end, or the Northern Corridor protocol between its states. This is the existing `roadRegimeOf`.
  2. **We carry for the airline, its handler or a forwarder under the AWB/HAWB**, e.g. the AWB names an off-airport departure or destination, or we truck between airports.
     - Art. 18(4) presumes damage occurred during air carriage, so MC99 governs the cargo owner's claim against the air carrier (26 SDR/kg, unbreakable).
     - Our liability to whoever hired us follows our contract with them, unless a court applies a road convention to that leg or lets the cargo owner sue us directly. That is a **lawyer question**.
  3. **The airline substitutes our truck for a planned flight** without the consignor's consent → "deemed to be within the period of carriage by air".
- **The handover moment is evidence.** The airline's charge begins at freight on hand: the Warehouse Receipt or FSU-FOH, then the Cargo Receipt or RCS. Our countersigned delivery note can serve as the Warehouse Receipt (Res. 672 Art. 2.15).

## 7. Advance cargo information for customs and security (fork §7; primary sources inside)

**EU ICS2**

*Who lodges the entry summary declaration (ENS)*
- **The carrier.** UCC 952/2013 Art. 127(4): "The entry summary declaration shall be lodged by the carrier." Others holding the particulars "may be required to provide" them (127(6)). Source: http://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/02013R0952-20221212
- **Air release.** ICS2 Release 2 started 1 Mar 2023, with deployment to 2 Oct 2023 (Implementing Decision 2023/2879). ICS2 has been "fully operational across all modes" since 1 June 2026.

*Time limits (DA 2015/2446 Art. 106)*
- Full ENS: by departure for flights under 4 hours; otherwise "four hours before the arrival".
- Pre-loading advance cargo information (PLACI): the minimum dataset "before the goods are loaded onto the aircraft on which they are to be brought into" the EU. That means the last leg, e.g. via Doha, Dubai or Addis.

*Multiple filing*
- A forwarder that does not pass house data up the chain must file it itself (DA Art. 113(1)), using an EU EORI (EU trader identification number).
- Each filer is responsible for what it submits (Art. 113a).
- The carrier must name parties that withheld data (IA 2015/2447 Art. 184(3)).

*Minimum dataset before loading (Annex B)*
- Consignor and consignee name and full address, goods description, packages, gross mass, HAWB (MAWB "if available").
- Identification number "where available". The HS code is optional at this stage.
- Before loading, the airline may get a request for information, a screening request ("High Risk Cargo and Mail") or a **do-not-load** order (IA Art. 186).

*Full ENS*
- Adds the 6-digit HS code (mandatory), the dangerous-goods UN number, place of acceptance, routing countries, flight number and other mode.

*Description quality*
- "General terms (i.e. 'consolidated', 'general cargo', 'parts' or 'freight of all kinds') … cannot be accepted".

**Kenya → EU flowers (e.g. JKIA → Amsterdam or Liège)**
- The airline files the master level.
- The consolidator either passes house data to the airline or files it itself.
- The grower files nothing but must supply true data.
- HS 0603.11 is "Roses".
- The UK list of unacceptable descriptions rejects "Plants, flowers, cuttings" and "Perishables" and accepts e.g. "Roses".

**US ACAS** (19 CFR 122.48b, eCFR current to 2026-10-27; amended by CBP Dec. 25-08)
- Due "no later than prior to loading". Full manifest data from Africa is due 4 h before arrival (122.48a).
- The carrier files unless an opt-in filer does; opting in needs a bond and a contact monitored 24/7.
- Data is at house level, including "shipment packing location and/or scheduled pickup location", i.e. our collection point.
- Generic descriptions ("FAK", "general cargo", "STC") are refused.
- On a do-not-load order, "The party with physical possession of the cargo will be required to carry out the Do-Not-Load (DNL) protocols".

**UK** (gov.uk ENS guidance)
- Great Britain uses the "S&S GB" service. Northern Ireland moved to ICS2 at the end of 2025; gov.uk gives two dates.
- Time limits are the same as the EU's (4 h before arrival for long haul).
- No air pre-loading rule was found for Great Britain; that is not proof there is none.

**China and India**
- China: GACC Decree 172 (2008). Inbound data before take-off (flights under 4 h) or 4 h before arrival, master and house waybills, power to order "not to load". Later amendments **not verified**.
- India: **not verified**.

**What we hold (synthesis):** structured parties with full addresses, the true consignor, a precise description, pieces, weight, 6-digit HS, UN number, consignee EORI, our pickup place coded, **who files the house data**, and the filer's MRN (the EU filing reference) as evidence. Never show "accepted by customs" ourselves.

## 8. National law (fork §8; primary sources inside)

**Kenya**
- **The statute.** The Carriage by Air Act No. 2 of 1993 (Cap. 394A) gives effect to Warsaw/Hague. Source: https://infotradekenya.go.ke/media/Carriage%20by%20Air%20Act%202%20of%201993.pdf

  - Uganda: CAA Act Cap 354 and the 2024 amendment.
  - Tanzania: Civil Aviation Act Cap 80, R.E. 2023.

## What this means for the design (synthesis)

1. **The air regime per lane is data.** Add an `AIR_INSTRUMENTS` table beside `ROAD_INSTRUMENTS`: MC99, MAP4, Hague, Warsaw 1929, Guadalajara. Each holds parties, dates, source URL and checkedOn, and limits are kept in their **native unit** (SDR or gold franc), never silently converted.
   - `airRegimeOf(departureCountry, destinationCountry, stopCountries, onDay)` returns `mc99 | warsaw_hague_map4 | warsaw_hague | warsaw_1929 | none | domestic(country)`.
   - Use the AWB's places of departure and destination (boxes 1/9 and 18) and stops (11A–F). For a house shipment, use the HAWB's places. Use the date "executed on" (32A).
   - A `domestic` result reads the per-country row from §8, each with a verified flag.
2. **The limit is dated data too:** 17 → 19 (2009-12-30) → 22 (2019-12-28) → 26 SDR/kg (2024-12-28).
   - Show "26 SDR × gross weight of the package(s) concerned (Art. 22(4))".
   - If box 16 holds an amount, show "limit = declared sum".
   - For MC99 and MAP4 lanes, state that the limit cannot be broken. Hague and Warsaw lanes say it can be (Art. 25).
   - Never compute a money figure in shillings unless we hold a conversion source (Art. 23: rate at the date of judgment).
3. **Clocks, like the export box clock (EX6):**
   - damage 14 days from receipt
   - delay 21 days from "placed at disposal"
   - written notice required
   - consignee may act after 7 days overdue (Art. 13(3))
   - 2-year bar from arrival, from when the aircraft ought to have arrived, or from when carriage stopped
   - Warsaw 1929 lanes: 7 and 14 days
   - Show the airline's contractual 120-day non-delivery notice as "the airline's terms", not law. Calendar days (Art. 52).
4. **A held AWB, "complete as printed":**
   - **Missing (the law asks for it on the document):**
     - MC99/MAP4: departure, destination, weight; plus a foreign stop if both ends are in one state.
     - Hague lane: also the **liability notice** (Art. 8(c)). If it is absent, show "the airline may not be able to limit its liability (Hague Art. 9)". This is a consequence, not a defect against the holder.
     - Warsaw 1929 lane: items (a)–(i) and (q) carry the same consequence.
     - Always add "the contract is still valid" (MC99 Art. 9).
   - **Ask (needed for the match, customs or release, but not required by MC99):**
     - shipper and consignee with full addresses; pieces; goods description and dimensions; declared values (16/17); handling information and UN number
     - prepaid or collect; issuing carrier and agent code; flight and date; signatures (31, 32C)
     - the consignor's original (Art. 12(3)) or, on e-AWB lanes, the Cargo Receipt (600g) or a printed shipment record
     - EAW/EAP and ECC/ECP codes; the HAWB↔MAWB link; who files ICS2/ACAS data, and the MRN
   - **"Wrong" (a hard error, not "missing"):** an AWB number whose last digit is not (first 7 digits of the serial) mod 7. A last digit of 7–9 is always wrong. HAWB numbers have no check digit.
   - **Match against our papers:**
     - pieces must match exactly, and gross weight within a tolerance; both are prima facie evidence (Art. 11(2))
     - the consignee must be the same party
     - our pickup and drop points must be consistent with the airports, and our handover time with "executed on" and freight on hand
     - the goods description must match and must not be vague (ICS2 list)
   - **Verified may only ever say:** "prefix maps to airline X (table, source)" and "the airline's tracking shows this master number". Everything else is "could not be verified". There is no public registry, and no airline tracking for house numbers.
5. **Is e-AWB feasible on this lane?** Yes when both ends are MC99 or both are MAP4 (IATA). Otherwise expect paper. On a MAP4 lane, electronic records need the consignor's consent (Art. 5(2)).
6. **Every road leg records "under whose contract":**
   - who booked our truck (shipper or consignee / forwarder / airline or ground handler)
   - whether an AWB or HAWB already covers the leg (off-airport place on the AWB, or a feeder flight number)
   - inside or outside the airport
   - Our own contract → road regime as today. Under the air contract → show the Art. 18(4) presumption, and Art. 30/43 if we act as the carrier's agent. Never invent a regime; where unclear, say "ask".
   - Record the handover: our countersigned delivery note meeting Res. 672's Shipper's Delivery Note minimum, then Warehouse Receipt / FSU-FOH, then Cargo Receipt.
7. **House and master:** hold both and link them.
   - Flag a declared value on the HAWB with "no value declared" on the MAWB: it does not bind the airline unless the airline agreed (Art. 41(2)).
   - Show that complaints may go to either carrier, but disposition instructions only to the contracting carrier (Art. 42).
8. **A hold state on air exports and imports.** A filer can report a referral or do-not-load while the cargo is on our truck. Record that instruction and stop the handover. We must not become an ACAS filer.
9. **Live animals, perishables, pharma:** surface the inherent-vice and defective-packing defences (Art. 18(2)(a)/(b)) next to the condition notes we record at pickup and handover. Our condition record is the evidence that matters.
10. **Copyright:** cite IATA resolution numbers; never embed IATA manual text. The airline prefix table needs a licensed or first-party source.
