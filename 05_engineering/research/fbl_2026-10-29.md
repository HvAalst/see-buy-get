# The FIATA FBL: what a platform that holds one should know and check

**The decision not to issue FBLs holds up, with one caveat.** It is a choice, not something forced on us. FIATA lets a forwarder in a country with no FIATA association get FBLs directly from FIATA as an Individual Member. Kenya has no FIATA member association, so that route is open there (FAQ Q7, §2). Doing so would still make us "a carrier" for the whole journey (FBL Definitions, cl. 2.1). It would also require liability insurance, and in India and Ethiopia a state registration or licence as a multimodal transport operator (MTO). So the decision stands.

## 1. The FBL itself

### Which edition is current
- **Conditions:** the current conditions are the **"Standard Conditions (1992)"**, on a form marked "© FIATA / Zurich-Switzerland 6.92". The form says it is "issued subject to UNCTAD / ICC Rules for Multimodal Transport Documents (ICC Publication 481)".
  - Source: FIATA *Documents and Forms* brochure (printed 08/2010) — https://fiata.cdn.prismic.io/fiata/afedd989-c99b-4142-9f64-8281c99aa4c5_FIATA_Documents_and_Forms.pdf
  - FIATA's **2023** *Guidance on Liability Insurance for the FBL* still reprints the "Standard Conditions (1992)" as its Annex 1 — https://fiata.cdn.prismic.io/fiata/300a60b6-eaad-4ee5-a0a4-9066d7fbf34e_LIG_2023.pdf
- **No 2018/2019 FBL revision found.** The October 2019 FIATA note I found revises the standard trading conditions and the Model Rules, not the FBL (a copy on a secondary host). Whether there is a newer FBL text: not verified.
- **The terms cannot be changed:** "The terms and conditions on the overleaf of FIATA documents cannot be altered" (FIATA FAQ Q12, https://fiata.org/international-law-trade-faq/; the answers sit in the page source).
- **The ICC logo:** it means the ICC "deemed" the form "in conformity with the UNCTAD/ICC Rules", but "does not imply ICC endorsement of the document" (form footer). This is the same stance as our "complete as printed, never valid".
- **The digital FBL (eFBL)** keeps the same conditions. FIATA's terms of use, Version 1.3 ("Valid as of 9 November 2022"), cl. 4.3: the user may not change "its terms and conditions" — https://fiata.cdn.prismic.io/fiata/c596e683-48bb-487a-800b-500c8e47791a_Digital+FBL+-+Terms+and+Conditions+-+Version+1.3.pdf

### The family of FIATA documents
Sources: the brochure, and FAQ Q1 (the documents associations may hand out: FCR, FCT, FWR, FBL plus digital FBL, FWB).

| Document | What it is | Relevant to holding papers? |
|---|---|---|
| **FBL** | Negotiable multimodal bill of lading (blue). The issuer is the carrier for the whole journey. Can also be issued as a sea bill of lading. | **Yes, the main one** |
| **FWB** | Non-negotiable multimodal waybill, 1997 conditions. "the FWB need not be presented by the consignee for the delivery of the goods". It has a box for "Transfer of right of control to consignee (Clause 4)". | **Yes.** Same liability limits (cl. 9.3, 9.5) |
| **FCR** | Forwarder's Certificate of Receipt: not negotiable, one original, "official acknowledgement" that the forwarder has the goods. Barred for steel since 2001 after forgeries and fraud (FAQ Q13). | Yes: a frequent instrument of fraud |
| **FCT** | Forwarder's Certificate of Transport. Negotiable "when made out 'To Order'". The forwarder answers as a forwarder, not as a carrier. | Yes, if users present one |
| **FWR** | FIATA Warehouse Receipt. Not negotiable unless marked so. | Marginal |
| **SDT** | Shipper's declaration for dangerous goods, filled in "by the shipper", never by the forwarder. Not accepted for rail under RID (the international rail dangerous-goods rules) or for air. | Yes, as a dangerous-goods check |
| **SIC** | Shipper's Intermodal Weight Certification. Rooted in US law, usable for the IMO's weighed-container (VGM) rule. | Yes, as a weight cross-check |
| **FFI** | FIATA Forwarding Instructions, on the UN layout key. | Yes: the shape of a shipper's letter of instruction |

### The boxes on the FBL's face (from the specimen itself)
- **Parties:** Consignor · "Consigned to order of" · Notify address
- **Route:** Place of receipt · Ocean vessel · Port of loading · Port of discharge · Place of delivery
- **Goods:**
  - Marks and numbers · Number and kind of packages · Description of goods · Gross weight · Measurement
  - These are marked "according to the declaration of the consignor"
- **Declarations:**
  - "Declaration of Interest of the consignor in timely delivery (Clause 6.2.)"
  - "Declared value for ad valorem rate according to the declaration of the consignor (Clauses 7 and 8)"
- **Fixed wording:**
  - "Taken in charge in apparent good order and condition, unless otherwise noted herein, at the place of receipt…"
  - The surrender clause (see negotiability below)
- **Money and paper:**
  - Freight amount · Freight payable at
  - Cargo insurance through the undersigned: "not covered / Covered according to attached Policy"
  - "Number of Original FBL's"
  - Place and date of issue · Stamp and signature
  - "For delivery of goods please apply to:"
- **Printed marks:** the emblem of the national association, the FBL number, and the ICC logo.
- **The eFBL's data model** carries the same boxes (https://github.com/FIATA/eFBL, schema release 20230106). It also has container identity (`transportEquipment.id`), `affixedSeal`, `tareWeight`, `grossWeight`, UN numbers for dangerous goods, and `originalIssuedQuantity`.
  - **Pitfall:** in the schema, `carrierAcceptanceLocation` is described as "Place of delivery" and `consigneeReceiptLocation` as "Place of receipt". The names look swapped against their labels. Read the printed label; FIATA's intent is not verified.

### Negotiability
- **cl. 3.1:** "This FBL is issued in a negotiable form unless it is marked 'non negotiable'. It shall constitute title to the goods and the holder, by endorsement of this FBL, shall be entitled to receive or to transfer the goods".
- **On the face:** "One of these Multimodal Transport Bills of Lading must be surrendered duly endorsed in exchange for the goods… one of which being accomplished the other(s) to be void."
- **Number of originals:** FIATA requires only "that it is clearly specified how many original FBLs are issued" (brochure, p.13). That "usually three" are issued is market practice; not verified from a FIATA source.
- **Colour:** "the only valid original FBL is blue… Any other FBL in a different colour will not be considered as an original" (FAQ Q11).
- **cl. 3.2:** the particulars are prima facie evidence, weakened by notes such as "shipper's weight, load and count" or "shipper-packed container". They become conclusive once the FBL has passed for value to a consignee who relied on it in good faith.

### Liability under the 1992 conditions (all verified against the text)
- **Who answers.** The Definitions say the forwarder is the MTO that "assumes liability for the performance of the multimodal transport contract as a carrier". cl. 2.1(a) says it "undertakes to perform and/or in his own name to procure the performance of the entire transport". cl. 2.2 makes it answer for its subcontractors.
- **cl. 6.1–6.2, the period and the fault rule:** the forwarder answers from taking in charge to delivery, on presumed fault. It answers for delay **only** if the consignor's declaration of interest in timely delivery was accepted "and stated in this FBL".
- **cl. 6.4:** after "ninety consecutive days" past the delivery date, the goods may be treated as lost.
- **cl. 6.6, sea defences:** errors in navigation or in managing the ship, and fire, are excused.
- **cl. 7, mandatory law first:** 7.1 gives way to mandatory conventions and national law. 7.2 applies the Hague or Hague-Visby Rules to sea and inland waterway legs; 7.3 applies US COGSA.
- **cl. 8.3, the main limit:** "666.67 SDR per package or unit or 2 SDR per kilogram of gross weight… whichever is the higher". A value declared and accepted before taking in charge becomes the limit instead.
- **cl. 8.4, what counts as a package:** packages "enumerated in the FBL" as packed in a container count one by one; otherwise the container is the package.
- **cl. 8.5, no water leg:** if the contract does not "include carriage of goods by sea or by inland waterways", the limit is **8.33 SDR/kg**.
- **cl. 8.6(a), the stage rule:** where the loss happened on a known stage, the limit of the "applicable international convention or mandatory national law" for that stage applies. **cl. 8.6(b):** US$500 per package where COGSA applies.
- **cl. 8.7, delay:** limited to "twice the freight under the multimodal contract".
- **cl. 8.8–8.9:** the total is capped at the total-loss limit. The limits are lost for intent, or recklessness with knowledge.
- **cl. 10, subcontractors:** the same limits protect "any servant, agent or other person (including any independent contractor)". That could include our road carriers (lawyer question below).
- **cl. 14:** a lien on the goods *and documents*.
- **cl. 16, notice:** given in writing at delivery. For damage that is not apparent, "within 6 consecutive days after the day when the goods were delivered". Missing the notice only makes delivery prima facie good; it does not bar the claim.
- **cl. 17, time limit:** suit must be brought "within 9 months after the delivery of the goods", or after the date it should have been delivered, or after the 90-day point in cl. 6.4.
- **cl. 19, where to sue:** "only in the place where the Freight Forwarder has his place of business as stated on the reverse of this FBL", under that country's law.
- **In money (useful for plain words):** IMF, 28 Oct 2026: "SDR1 = US$1.351780" (https://www.imf.org/external/np/fin/data/rms_sdrv.aspx). So 666.67 SDR ≈ US$901 per package, 2 SDR/kg ≈ US$2.70/kg, and 8.33 SDR/kg ≈ US$11.26/kg.
- **FIATA's worked example:** 22,000 kg of marble comes to at most "44,000SDR", and 300 packages to at most "200,001SDR" (LIG 2023, p.8).

### UCP 600 and what a bank checks
- **What FIATA claims:** the FBL "conforms to the requirements of… (UCP 600)… when issued as multimodal transport document in line with Article 19 or as bill of lading in line with Article 20". The FWB conforms under Art. 21 as a sea waybill (brochure).
- **UCP 600 Art. 19(a)** (reproduction of the ICC text: https://cpeapp.icai.org/downloadBGM/65c1c211b0373.pdf). A transport document covering at least two modes, "however named, must appear to":
  - (i) name the carrier, and be signed by the carrier, the master, or a named agent, with the signature "identified as that of the carrier, master or agent";
  - (ii) show dispatch, taking in charge or shipment at the place in the credit;
  - (iii) show the place of dispatch, taking in charge or shipment, and the final destination;
  - (iv) be the sole original, or the "full set as indicated on the transport document";
  - (v) carry or refer to the terms of carriage, whose contents "will not be examined";
  - (vi) contain "no indication that it is subject to a charter party".
- **Other UCP rules that matter:**
  - Art. 14(l): a transport document may be issued by "any party other than a carrier" if it meets Arts. 19–24.
  - Art. 14(a): banks decide "on the basis of the documents alone".
  - Art. 14(c): documents must be presented within 21 days of shipment.
  - Art. 27: the document must be "clean".
- **The credit decides which article applies.** ICC Banking Commission Doc 470/1128rev (2010): a port-to-port "bill of lading" credit is "to be examined against article 20". For pre-carriage by road or rail plus sea, the credit should call for Art. 19 — https://library.iccwbo.org/tfb/ps/Recommendations-of-the-Banking-Commission-in-respect-of-the-requirements-for-an-On-board-Notation.pdf
- **ICC Opinion TA 572, on an FBL:** a bank "would be obliged to accept a bill of lading that was signed 'as carrier' irrespective of any knowledge it may have as to the capacity of the issuer" (DCInsight 16/3, https://library.iccwbo.org/content/tfb/dcinsight/DCI_Vol16n3_TransportDocuments.htm).
- **eUCP 2.1**, ICC's rules for electronic presentation, aligned with MLETR (the UN model law on electronic transferable records; see §2): https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2022/03/823E_eUCP_FInal.pdf
- The ISBP 821 paragraphs are behind a paywall: not verified.

## 2. Who may issue an FBL, and how to check it is genuine

### FIATA's rules on issuing
- **Who hands them out:** "The authority to control printing and distribution of our documents is restricted to the member organisations of FIATA". Associations mark the forms "with a suffix letter corresponding to the UN country codes". They "maintain a register of member firms who have received copies of the documents, indicating their serial number". "Reproduction of the documents by individual firms in any form is strictly prohibited." (Brochure, General Stipulations 1, 2, 4, 5.)
- **An association needs an agreement first:** it may hand out documents only under a Distribution Agreement with FIATA, valid one year at a time after a compliance review (FAQ Q1, Q3, Q5).
- **Its checks before handing out:** an association must "perform a verification check on the company requesting the documents, and to keep a register of all companies… together with the unique serial number of each document". For the FBL, digital FBL and FWB it must also make sure the company "holds appropriate liability insurance" (FAQ Q4).
- **One country only:** an FBL "can only be issued by the Individual Member in the territory of the FIATA Association Member that distributed it, and cannot be used by branches or subsidiaries… in other territories".
- **Where there is no association:** FIATA Individual Members can get documents "directly with FIATA" under a User Agreement with proof of insurance (FAQ Q7).
- **Insurance:** "Freight forwarders issuing FBLs have to insure their liability in accordance with the FBL conditions" (brochure). All issuers "are required to be in possession of valid liability insurance" (LIG 2023, p.4).
- **Conditions for issuing the digital FBL** (terms of use cl. 5.1):
  - a FIATA Digital Identity;
  - "valid liability insurance";
  - "valid membership with a freight forwarding association that is a valid FIATA member";
  - national legal requirements.

  Note: FIATA's digital FBL page also allows FIATA Individual Members to issue — https://fiata.org/digital-bill-of-lading/
- **Model Rules:** the FIATA Model Rules for Freight Forwarding Services (1996) are voluntary forwarding terms. They differ from the FBL conditions, which "cover solely the carriage of goods under the FBL". Where both apply, "the limit of carrier's liability in the FBL is higher… and such higher limit should apply" — https://fiata.cdn.prismic.io/fiata/1d11e01d-a486-417f-83e1-99f5c283431a_STCs_and_FBL_TCs.pdf

### Checking a paper FBL
FIATA publishes **no public check** for a paper FBL. Its tests come from the rules above:
- the original is blue (FAQ Q11);
- it carries the association's emblem and country-code letter;
- it carries a serial number that should be in **that association's register** against the issuing firm (General Stipulations 2 and 4; FAQ Q4);
- it carries the "Text authorized by FIATA… 6.92" mark.

Other security features (watermark, paper): not published, not verified.

So the practical route is to ask the association whose emblem is printed. Their contacts are in FIATA's directory. FIATA itself "is neither an arbitrator nor a legal firm" (FAQ Q15).

### The electronic FBL (eFBL)
- **Launch:** FIATA's release of **18 May 2022**, reprinted by AJOT: https://www.ajot.com/news/fiata-launches-paperless-fbl-solution-allowing-freight-forwarders-to-issue-secured-paperless-fbl-through-their-everyday-tms-and-alternative-software-tools
- **How it is issued:** forwarders buy packages from FIATA and issue through software from a licensed vendor. About 46 vendors are listed at https://www.efbl.fiata.org/ (CargoWise, CargoX, Bolero, Magaya, Descartes, DP World and others).
- **The ledger:** Komgo's "Trakk" runs it. The FAQ says "A private, centralized Quantum Ledger Data Base (QLDB) blockchain is used" (https://www.efbl.fiata.org/faq). AWS ended support for QLDB on 31 Jul 2025 (AWS developer guide notice), so what runs today is not verified. FIATA updated the verification widget in Oct 2025 (GitHub).
- **Numbering:** "A specific numbering methodology and sequence will be allocated automatically" (terms cl. 4.4).
- **What the QR code shows:** the issuer's "company name and address, business registration number and name of the association" (cl. 7.4).
- **Drafts and amendments** (https://github.com/FIATA/eFBL/blob/main/docs/PAPERLESS_FBL_SERVICE_USAGE.md):
  - "Draft versions are not registered and cannot be verified".
  - Issued documents can be verified "as long as it is not deactivated".
  - "Previous versions of an amended document will not be verifiable anymore and thus considered as invalid".
- **The official way to verify:**
  - **For people:** https://fiata.org/document-verification/ (also https://fiata.github.io/eFBL/verification-page/). Upload the PDF, or type the document ID ("top right of the document"), or scan the QR code on a paper print.
  - **What it returns:** whether the document "was correctly registered by FIATA", who the issuer is, and "a snapshot of the document when it was issued".
  - **A negative result** means it "has not been registered by FIATA" or has been altered.
- **No public verification API is documented.** The page is a Komgo browser widget calling `https://api.komgo.io/api/magic-link`, which is not documented for third parties. The API in FIATA's repo is for *issuing*, and needs FIATA credentials. We should link out, not call it.
- **The check does not cover title.** "FIATA does not interfere in the transfer of title process". Electronic transferable records are "not yet legally recognized worldwide" (eFBL FAQ). "FIATA shall not be responsible for any matters whatsoever pertaining to the transferability or negotiability of the Digital FBL" (terms cl. 13.2).
  - So the check says nothing about who holds the document now, or about endorsements or surrender. The eFBL data model has no holder or endorsement fields.
- **Other bills pass the same check:** FIATA's service also produces "the non-FIATA branded Bill of Lading" (terms cl. 1.11). A positive check therefore does not by itself mean "FBL under the FIATA conditions".
- **Insurance:** "Digital FBLs packages do not include any insurance coverage" (eFBL FAQ). A 2026 WTW / HCC Tokio Marine "Insured eFBL" programme now exists (deck linked from https://fiata.org/digital-bill-of-lading/).

### The law behind electronic negotiable documents
- **MLETR:** UNCITRAL's status page says "adopted in 13 States and a total of 13 jurisdictions" — https://uncitral.un.org/en/texts/ecommerce/modellaw/electronic_transferable_records/status
  - The 13: Bahrain 2018; Belize, Kiribati, Paraguay, Singapore and Abu Dhabi Global Market 2021; Papua New Guinea 2022; UK 2023; France and Timor-Leste 2024; China (bills of lading only), Marshall Islands and Mauritius (bills of exchange only) 2025.
  - **Kenya and the rest of East Africa are absent.**
- **UK:** the Electronic Trade Documents Act 2023 has been in force since 20.9.2023 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/38/section/8).
- **Germany** (not MLETR): HGB §443(3) and §516(2) treat an equivalent electronic record like a paper bill.
- **US** (not MLETR): UCC §7-106, "control" of an electronic document of title.
- **Kenya:** the Kenya Information and Communications Act, Cap. 411A (Rev. 2011), s.83B excludes "negotiable instruments" and "documents of title" from its electronic-writing rules. Whether the Business Laws (Amendment) Act 2020 changed this: not verified.
- **Uganda:** the Electronic Transactions Act 2011, Schedule 2(e), excludes "Negotiable instruments, including negotiable documents of title".
- **Tanzania:** the Electronic Transactions Act 2015 has no such exclusion, but also no rules on control of an electronic record.
- **Accra Convention on Negotiable Cargo Documents:**
  - Adopted by UN General Assembly resolution A/RES/80/162 on 15 Dec 2025 (https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4097760/files/A_RES_80_162-EN.pdf).
  - Opened for signature in Accra on **26 Oct 2026**. UN Treaty Collection X-22, "STATUS AS AT : 28-10-2026": 26 signatories, no ratifications, not in force (https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=X-22&chapter=10&clang=_en).
  - Of our countries, **Tanzania, Rwanda, DR Congo, South Sudan, Djibouti and China** have signed. Kenya, Uganda and Ethiopia have not.
  - It enters into force 180 days after the 10th ratification (Art. 25).
  - It covers only documents with "a conspicuous reference to this Convention" (Art. 1(1)), which today's FBL does not have.
  - A "transport operator" includes whoever "assumes responsibility for the performance of the contract" (Art. 2(8)), so a forwarder acting as MTO counts.
  - Art. 3(5) speaks to the house-bill versus master-bill question.
- **ICC Digital Standards Initiative, Key Trade Documents** (https://www.digitalizetrade.org/ktdde): lists the bill of lading, sea waybill, CIM and CMR, but no separate multimodal document and no FBL.
- **Rotterdam Rules:** 5 parties, not in force.

## 3. East Africa and the rest, country by country

### FIATA associations and the directory
- **The directory:** https://fiata.org/directory/ has one page per country (e.g. `/directory/ke/`). Each page lists "Association Members", "Individual Members" (forwarders) and "Affiliate Members", with name, address, phone and website.
  - There is no API.
  - Some entries are drawn greyed out (`opacity-50 grayscale`), and the page does not say why. Lapsed membership is possible but not verified.
- **Associations offering the digital FBL:** https://www.fiata.org/associations-digital-fbls/ lists 31. Of ours, only SAAFF (South Africa), FFFAI (India), NAFL (UAE), FENEX (Netherlands), VHSP (Germany) and BIFA (UK). **No East African association is on it.**

### Laws requiring an MTO licence (only India, Ethiopia, the Andean Community and ASEAN make it a precondition)
- **India — Multimodal Transportation of Goods Act 1993:**
  - s.3: "No person shall carry on or commence the business of multimodal transportation unless he is registered under this Act".
  - Registration is valid 3 years and renewable (s.4(4), as amended in 2000). An MTO needs valid insurance before issuing a document (s.7).
  - Its limits match the FBL's: s.14 is 2 SDR/kg or 666.67 SDR per package, and 8.33 SDR/kg with no water leg. Notice is 6 days (s.20); suit within 9 months (s.24).
  - **Public register:** DG Shipping's "List of the Registered MTOs" (XLSX dated 17 Jun 2026, about 2,546 rows; numbers shaped like MTO/DGS/n/MON/YYYY ending in the expiry date, with a status) — https://dgma.gov.in/multimodal-transport-operation/mto-list-of-the-registered-mtos
  - The section wording comes from bare-act copies (https://bnblegal.com/bareact/multimodal-transportation-goods-act-1993/), because India Code blocked access.
- **Ethiopia:**
  - Proclamation 549/2007 Art. 6(7): the Maritime Affairs Authority shall "issue license to persons desiring to engage in multi modal transport business" (https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2007/pr_549_2007.pdf).
  - Proclamation 548/2007 sets **different limits from the FBL**: Art. 20 has 835 SDR per package or 2.5 SDR/kg; Art. 21 has 2.5 times the freight for delay; Art. 40 has a 2-year time bar (https://www.metaappz.com/pdf/ethiopian_laws/federal/2007/pr_548_2007.pdf).
  - The ESLSE (Ethiopian Shipping and Logistics) monopoly was opened by a 2021 directive. The first private MTOs were licensed in Mar 2024. Directive 1140/2026 is reported in the press; its text was not seen.
- **Kenya:** no MTO licensing found.
  - Customs agents are licensed under EAC Customs Management Act s.145(1): "The Commissioner may license persons to act as agents…".
  - KRA's renewal notice (21/09/2026) asks for a "KIFWA Clearance Certificate" — https://www.kra.go.ke/news-center/public-notices/2398-application-for-renewal-of-customs-agents-licences-2027
  - The Merchant Shipping (Maritime Transport Operators) Regulations 2024 (LN 92/2024): which classes they license, and whether they are still in force, are disputed and not verified.
- **Tanzania:** TASAC licenses and registers "clearing and forwarding agents" under the Shipping Agencies Act 2017, s.12(1)(a) and (k). It keeps a public register (s.55). The Act has no MTO provision. https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/tan216553.pdf
- **Uganda:** no statute found. Customs agents are licensed by URA under s.145.
- **Rwanda:** RURA issues a "License for Freight Forwarders" (https://www.rura.rw/sectors/transport/sub-sectors-and-services/freight-forwarders-service). No MTO law found.
- **EAC:** Treaty Art. 95 asks member states to harmonise multimodal documents and to "ratify or accede to international conventions on multimodal transport". Art. 97 asks them to harmonise forwarder licensing (https://rtais.wto.org/rtadocs/94/TOA/English/EAC%20TREATY.pdf). No EAC or COMESA multimodal instrument was found.
- **ASEAN Framework Agreement 2005:**
  - Art. 29: an MTO "shall be registered with the competent national body". Art. 30 sets the conditions, including insurance.
  - Limits as in the FBL; nine-month time bar.
  - Copy: https://lawforasean.ocs.go.th/File/files/1473714825.54401f43fe91ed75a0d7d999dbcadafb.pdf
- **Andean Community:**
  - Decision 331, Art. 29: an MTO must be "inscrito en el registro respectivo" (entered in that country's register).
  - Decision 393, Art. 13: the registration certificate lasts five years. Limits as in the FBL.
  - Copy: https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/1867360/Decisi%C3%B3n%20N%C2%B0%20331.pdf
- **Mercosur** (CMC Decision 15/94) and **Brazil's Lei 9.611/1998**: not verified.
- **1980 UN Multimodal Convention:** "Not yet in force". It needs 30 states and has 11 parties, including **Rwanda (1987), Burundi (1998)**, Malawi and Zambia (https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XI-E-1&chapter=11&clang=_en). The UNCTAD/ICC Rules apply only by contract, as the FBL does.

### Release at the port, and the house bill against the master bill
- **No Kenyan statute was found** that requires an original to be surrendered at Mombasa. It is carrier practice plus the general law of bills of lading.
- **MSC's own procedure deck** (undated; hosted by UFFA, https://www.ugandafreightforwarders.com/publication/view/shipping-line-presentation-ug):
  - a to-order bill needs the endorsements of the "Shipper… Bank… Consignee… Uganda Agent… Mombasa Agent", or an "Email of release (If Surrendered in K'la)";
  - charges are paid after "presenting the OBL" (the original bill of lading);
  - "The Kenyan agent signs a guarantee form"; the delivery order goes "to KPA via EDI";
  - alternatives: "Telex Release: OBLs have been surrendered at POL", and "Seaway Non Negotiable: Where no OBLs were issued".
- **How the FBL fits (my synthesis):** the FBL is the forwarder's *house* document to its customer. The shipping line releases only against *its own* master bill, telex release or waybill, which the forwarder or its agent controls. The FBL holder therefore surrenders the FBL to the agent named under "For delivery of goods please apply to:". That agent then obtains the line's delivery order.

## 4. What a platform holding an FBL should check (my synthesis)

**A. Complete as printed**
- Every box in §1 is filled.
- The document title says FBL or FWB, the form carries "6.92" or "10.97", and the ICC logo and association emblem are present.
- A number of originals is stated.
- The place and date of issue are given.
- It carries a stamp and signature, with the capacity stated ("as carrier" or "as agent for [named carrier]", per UCP Art. 19(a)(i)).
- There is no charter-party wording.
- Any clauses noting damage are recorded, as are "shipper's load and count" style notes (cl. 3.2).

**B. Agrees with our papers** (show each as "matches / differs / not on the FBL")
- **Container number and seals:** compare with our box record. A seal changed by customs is normal and should be explained.
- **Weight:** compare the FBL gross weight with our weighed mass (VGM) minus the tare weight.
- **Packages:** the count and kind. This drives the limit through cl. 8.4.
- **Place of receipt against where our truck took the box:**
  - If the place of receipt is inland, our road leg is inside the FBL and the forwarder answers for it (cl. 2.2).
  - If it is the port, our leg is outside.
- **Taking in charge against our hand-over time:** an FBL dated *before* the forwarder had the goods is a red flag. FIATA tells issuers to ensure "he/she has taken over the goods".
- **Ports and places:** port of loading, port of discharge and place of delivery against our booking.
- **Parties:** consignee and notify party against our parties. Under a to-order bill, "Consigned to order of [bank]" with the buyer as the notify party is normal; that is not a mismatch.
- **Dangerous goods:** UN numbers against the shipper's dangerous-goods declaration.
- **Country:** the issuer's country should match the association country code printed on the form.

**C. Is it genuine?**
- **eFBL:** send the user to FIATA's page, and record "FIATA's verification page showed: registered, issuer X, ID Y, on [date]". Check again before any moment that matters, because amendments invalidate earlier versions.
- **Issuer:** is it in fiata.org/directory for its country (as an Association Member's member or an Individual Member)? Flag greyed entries.
- **Associations' own lists:**
  - BIFA member search: https://www.bifa.org/membership/member-search/
  - TAFFA list: https://taffa.or.tz/taffa-members-list/
- **India:** the issuer must also be on DG Shipping's MTO register.
- **Paper:** "the serial number can only be confirmed by [association]". We cannot see the colour reliably on a scan.

**D. Warnings about negotiability**
- How many originals exist, and that **delivery is only against one surrendered, duly endorsed**.
- After one original is surrendered, the others are void.
- **A scan or PDF held by us is not an original.**
- An eFBL's title transfer sits outside FIATA's system. In Kenya and Uganda the electronic-transactions laws expressly exclude documents of title (Kenya on the 2011 text).

**E. To explain in plain words**
- The limits: cl. 8.3 or 8.5, converted at the day's SDR rate.
- The stage rule (cl. 8.6(a)). For a road-plus-sea FBL, a loss on our truck is likely capped at **2 SDR/kg, not 8.33**, because the contract includes sea. No East African state is in CMR, which matches your own CMR finding.
- Delay is covered only if declared (cl. 6.2) and is capped at twice the freight (cl. 8.7).
- The 90-day "treat as lost" rule (cl. 6.4).
- Notice at delivery, or within 6 days for hidden damage (cl. 16).
- The 9-month time bar (cl. 17).
- Where to sue (cl. 19).
- The liability limits are not cargo insurance (LIG 2023).
- Mandatory local law can override the printed terms (cl. 7.1). Ethiopia's Proclamation 548/2007, for example, has different figures.

**What could make "hold, check and explain" risky or misleading**
- A green tick reads as vouching. A forged FBL can copy our container number and seal exactly, so "agrees with our papers" proves nothing about genuineness. Show facts and their sources, never "valid".
- FIATA's check proves registration, an unchanged snapshot and the issuer's identity at the time of issue. It does **not** prove:
  - the current holder or the endorsements;
  - that an original has not already been surrendered;
  - that the goods were really taken in charge;
  - that the issuer's insurance or membership is still current.
- A positive check can also come from a "non-FIATA branded" bill.
- **Copyright and brand:** never render an FBL lookalike or a template (General Stipulation 5), and never use FIATA's logo (https://fiata.org/use-of-fiata-brand/).
- **Do not automate the undocumented Komgo calls.**
- **Banks:** the credit decides, under Art. 19 or Art. 20. We must never say "acceptable under your credit".
- **Your existing rule** that a receiver's copy has no carriage price: the FBL image carries "Freight amount", so decide who may see the image.
- **Resilience:** keep our own frozen, hashed copy and what FIATA's page said. Do not depend on FIATA's ledger lasting, given the QLDB end of support.
